# Rieke Septic Service: Homepage Conversion Breakdown

## The goal of this page

Right now, a homeowner in Dundee who searches "septic pumping near me" finds Rieke only on directory sites. There is no website to land on, so the call often goes to whichever competitor has one.

This homepage has one job: turn that search into a phone call or a booked pumping date.

Everything on it is written for one person: the homeowner. Usually they are in one of four moments:

1. **Panic.** Sewage is backing up and they need someone now.
2. **Routine.** They know it has been a few years and want it handled.
3. **Deadline.** They are buying or selling a home and need pumping or an inspection before closing.
4. **Unsure.** Something smells or looks wrong and they do not know what it means.

Each section below is there to move one or more of these people closer to calling.

## Section by section

### 1. Top strip: "Sewage backing up? Call now"

**What it is:** A thin dark bar at the very top with the phone number.

**Why it converts:** The panicked homeowner does not read. They scan for a phone number. This puts the number in the first place their eyes land, with words that match their exact problem. On mobile, the number is tappable.

### 2. Header with phone number and "Book Pumping" button

**What it is:** Logo, short menu, phone number, and a bright button. It stays pinned to the top as the visitor scrolls.

**Why it converts:** Wherever the visitor is on the page, the next step is one tap away. They never have to scroll back up to find how to reach you.

### 3. Hero: headline, two buttons, and a short form

**What it is:** The headline "Septic Pumping in Dundee and the Fox Valley, Done Right the First Time", a call button, a secondary button, three short trust points, and a 4-question form.

**Why it converts:**

- **The headline says the service and the town.** The visitor knows in under 3 seconds they are in the right place. It also helps Google match the page to "septic pumping Dundee" searches.
- **Two ways to act.** People who want to talk tap "Call". People who would rather not call fill in the form. Offering both captures more of both groups.
- **The form only asks 4 questions.** Name, phone, town, and what they need. Every extra field loses people. Four is the limit that still gives you enough to call them back with a price.
- **The "What do you need?" dropdown pre-sorts the lead.** You know before you call back whether it is an emergency, a routine pump, or a home sale.
- **Trust points sit right under the buttons.** "Local to Dundee", "Rated on Checkbook.org", "Fair, upfront pricing" answer the first doubt (can I trust these people?) at the moment of decision.

### 4. "Which one sounds like you?"

**What it is:** Four cards: backup, routine, home sale, and not sure. Each has its own button.

**Why it converts:** It lets the visitor pick their own situation instead of reading the whole page. The emergency card is red and goes straight to a phone call. The routine and home-sale cards go to the booking form. The "not sure" card invites a call to ask a question, which is the easiest first step for someone who is hesitant.

### 5. Services

**What it is:** Six short service boxes: pumping, inspections, repairs, emergencies, real estate pumping, and drain field checks.

**Why it converts:** It confirms "yes, they do the thing I need." Each box describes the outcome for the homeowner, not just the job name. It also gives Google more words to rank the page for different septic searches.

### 6. Warning signs

**What it is:** A dark section listing six signs a tank needs attention, with a call button.

**Why it converts:** Many visitors are not sure whether they have a problem yet. When they see their own symptom on the list ("gurgling pipes", "wet grass over the tank"), they move from "maybe later" to "I should call now." The line about a pump-out costing far less than a new drain field gives them a money reason to act early.

### 7. Why homeowners call Rieke, with owner photo

**What it is:** A real photo of the owner with the truck, and four reasons to choose Rieke.

**Why it converts:** People let you onto their property and trust you with an unpleasant job. Seeing a real face from a local business builds that trust faster than any logo. The four reasons answer the most common fears in this trade: getting a call center, getting upsold, surprise pricing, and an out-of-area company.

**Needed from you:** One photo of the owner or crew with the truck. A phone photo is fine.

### 8. How it works: 3 steps

**What it is:** Call or send the form, we pump and check, you get a clear report.

**Why it converts:** People hesitate when they cannot picture what happens next. Three simple steps remove that uncertainty and make booking feel low-effort.

### 9. Reviews

**What it is:** A real review from Checkbook.org ("Always reliable and trustworthy. Fair pricing.") and a box showing the Google rating and review count.

**Why it converts:** Homeowners trust other homeowners more than they trust the business. A short quote with a nearby town (Carpentersville) feels local and real. The rating box gives a fast number to trust.

**Needed from you:** Your Google rating and number of reviews. If you do not have a Google Business Profile set up yet, that is the first thing we would fix, because it is where most local calls come from.

### 10. Pumping reminder

**What it is:** A yellow band where the homeowner leaves their mobile number and when they last pumped. You text them once when they are due.

**Why it converts:** Not every visitor needs you today. This catches the ones who would otherwise leave and forget. It turns them into a repeat customer list you own, which brings them back to you in 2 or 3 years instead of to a competitor.

### 11. Service area with map

**What it is:** A list of nearby towns and a map pinned on Huntley Road.

**Why it converts:** The first question a homeowner asks is "do they come to my town?" Seeing their town listed answers it without a phone call. The town names also help the page show up when people search with their own town name.

### 12. FAQ

**What it is:** Eight common questions with short answers.

**Why it converts:** Each question is a reason someone might not call: "How much will it cost?", "Do I need to be home?", "I do not know where my lid is." Answering them on the page removes the objection before it stops the call. The price answer is honest. It explains what affects the price and promises a quote on the phone, which pushes the visitor to call.

### 13. Final call to action

**What it is:** A last green band with the call button and the booking button.

**Why it converts:** Visitors who read to the bottom are interested. This gives them the next step right where they finish, instead of making them scroll back up.

### 14. Footer

**What it is:** Phone, address, mailing address, hours, and license number.

**Why it converts:** Full contact details and a license number show this is a real, established business. It also matches the business details Google sees elsewhere, which helps local search ranking.

### Mobile: sticky "Call Now" and "Book Pumping" bar

**What it is:** On phones, two buttons stay fixed at the bottom of the screen the whole time.

**Why it converts:** Most septic searches happen on a phone, often while something is going wrong. One thumb tap calls you, from any point on the page.

## Things built in that you do not see

- **Business details for Google.** The page includes hidden business info (name, phone, address, service towns) that Google reads to show you in local results and on the map.
- **Fast loading.** No heavy images or plugins, so it loads quickly on a phone signal.
- **Clickable phone number everywhere.** Every phone number on the page dials directly on mobile.
