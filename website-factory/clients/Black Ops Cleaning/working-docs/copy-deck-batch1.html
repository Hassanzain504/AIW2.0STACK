Block 03, what the work covers

What the work covers

Everything below is part of a standard remediation. Nothing here is added to the bill later.

Assessment and containment

The affected area is isolated before anything is touched, with negative air where the contamination warrants it, so nothing spreads through the rest of the property.

Removal of affected material

Flooring, subfloor, drywall, insulation and furnishings that cannot be safely restored are removed and documented. Decomposition travels further than most people expect, which is why the assessment happens before the price.

Decontamination

Hospital grade disinfection of every affected surface, following CDC and EPA disinfection guidance.

Odour remediation at source

Treated at the source rather than masked. Masking is the reason odour comes back three weeks later, and it is the most common complaint about work done cheaply.

Regulated waste disposal

Transported and disposed of through a licensed medical waste stream. The manifest is handed to you.

Written clearance

Confirmation that the property is safe for occupancy, in writing, on completion.

Block 06, insurance

Who pays for this

In most cases, the property insurance does. This is the question we are asked before any other, so here is the straight answer.

Most homeowner policies treat unattended death remediation as a covered loss, because the damage to the property is what is being claimed for. Coverage is not universal though, and there are two things worth knowing before you call your insurer.

  • Some policies exclude deaths by suicide unless a specific rider was bought. If that applies, we will tell you early rather than late.
  • Ask the adjuster, not the agent. The agent sold the policy. The adjuster is the person who decides what it pays, and they can answer in one call.

Ask them two questions: does my policy cover biohazard remediation after a death, and what is the limit. That is enough for us to work with.

What we do from there. We speak to the adjuster directly, provide the scope, the photographs and the documentation they need, and bill them. You are not paying up front and claiming it back.

If the loss is not covered, we tell you that before we start work, not after.

Block 07, credentials

Why we are allowed to do this work

Biohazard remediation is not general cleaning, and the difference is not marketing. It is training, insurance and a licensed disposal route.

  • GBAC trained. The Global Biorisk Advisory Council, now an ISSA division. This is the standard facility managers look for when they need certified infection control.
  • ISSA CMI. The Cleaning Management Institute, the industry's training body.
  • OSHA bloodborne pathogen compliant. Not a certificate anyone issues, a standard every technician is trained against.
  • Licensed and insured in Maryland, general liability and workers compensation.
  • Veteran owned and federally registered. SAM.gov UEI PMPPFTB7CCX4.

One thing we will not do

We do not display CDC, EPA or OSHA seals to imply those agencies endorse us. They do not endorse anyone. We follow CDC and EPA disinfection guidance and our technicians are trained to the OSHA bloodborne pathogen standard. Those are facts, and they are worth more than a logo. If another company shows you federal seals, ask them what they mean.

Block 11, FAQ

Questions people actually ask

Ten questions. This block is what AI answers pull from, and it is where every competitor in this market is thin. PuroClean's Rockville biohazard page has four. Bio-One's DC page has two.

Does homeowner insurance cover unattended death cleanup?

In most cases yes, because you are claiming for damage to the property. Some policies exclude deaths by suicide unless a rider was bought. Call your adjuster, not your agent, and ask whether the policy covers biohazard remediation and what the limit is. We handle the claim from there.

Will your vehicle be marked?

No. Our response vehicles carry no company signage and our technicians arrive in plain clothing and change on site. Neighbours see a plain van.

How quickly can you get here?

Within two hours across Montgomery County. Rockville, Bethesda and Silver Spring are usually faster, because we are based on Talbott Street in Rockville rather than driving in from another county.

Do I have to be at the property?

No. We can work from a key handover or a lockbox and send you photographic documentation as we go. Many of the people who call us live in another state.

Can the property be lived in or sold afterwards?

Yes, and we issue written clearance confirming it. That document is what insurers, buyers and estate solicitors ask to see, and it is included at no extra cost.

What if the odour comes back?

It should not. Odour that returns means the source was masked rather than removed, usually because subfloor or drywall was left in place. We treat at source, and if it recurs on a job we completed we come back at no charge.

Do I need to tell a buyer what happened here?

Maryland disclosure rules are a question for your solicitor or estate agent, not for us. What we can tell you is that our written clearance documents the property as remediated, which is what most buyers and their lenders actually want to see.

Who is responsible for arranging this, legally?

The property owner, or the executor of the estate. Police and the medical examiner secure the scene and then release the property. Cleanup is never their responsibility, which is a surprise to almost everyone who calls us.

Do you handle the waste paperwork?

Yes. Regulated waste goes through a licensed medical waste stream and the manifest is handed to you with the clearance documentation.

Are you police approved or state certified?

There is no such approval or certification to hold in Maryland, and any company claiming one is worth a second look. What matters is GBAC training, OSHA bloodborne pathogen compliance, a licensed disposal route, and being licensed and insured in Maryland. We are all four.

Block 03, local hook

Why cleaning in Rockville is not generic office cleaning

The most important block on the page. Every fact below is checkable and none of it survives a find and replace to another town.

Rockville sits at the centre of the I-270 Biotech Corridor, and that changes what a cleaning contract here actually has to do.

Over 120 of Montgomery County's roughly 350 life sciences firms have a Rockville address. More than 10,000 people work in biohealth in the private sector along the corridor, alongside an estimated 49,000 in federal agencies, with the National Institutes of Health and the Food and Drug Administration on the doorstep. Rockville supports over 73,000 jobs in total, and more than 200 IT companies sit on the same corridor.

What that means in practice is that a large share of the buildings here are not open plan offices. They are labs, GMP manufacturing space, medical suites and federal adjacent facilities. Those buildings come with rules an ordinary janitorial contract does not cover:

  • Controlled areas you do not enter without protocol. Cleaning around a lab is not the same job as cleaning a lab, and the contract has to say which.
  • Inspection schedules that decide when you can work. Medical and research facilities run to audit calendars, not to your rota.
  • Documented infection control, because a failed inspection costs a practice far more than a cleaning contract.
  • Background checked crews for federal adjacent buildings.

Rockville's older stock has the opposite problem. Town Center and the Pike corridor carry buildings from several decades of construction, and a 1980s office fitout does not clean like a 2020s lab building. Both are within ten minutes of us.

Block 04, what is different here

What is different about biohazard work in Rockville

The entire reason this page exists. Three or four paragraphs, no more.

The property mix changes the job. Rockville runs from garden apartments in Twinbrook to high rise condos in Town Center to detached homes in Fallsgrove, and each one changes how we contain a scene. A condo means shared ventilation, a shared lift and a building manager who has to be told something. A detached home means we can work without anyone on the street knowing.

Who calls us here is different too. In a town this dense with rental and managed property, a large share of these calls come from property managers and estate executors rather than from the family. Both need documentation more than they need reassurance: a scope, a disposal manifest and a written clearance they can put in a file.

Shared buildings need a plan before we arrive. In a managed building we agree the route in, the lift we use and the timing with the manager first, so that equipment is never carried through a lobby at 5pm. That single call is the difference between a discreet job and one the whole building talks about.

The corridor adds a category most towns do not have. With this many labs and medical facilities in Rockville, some of our biohazard calls here are commercial: a sharps incident, a spill in a treatment room, a contamination event that has to be documented for an inspection rather than for an insurer.
