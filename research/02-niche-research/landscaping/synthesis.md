NICHE SYNTHESIS, landscaping

Score from Module 2A: pending (complete /score-niches to populate)

End customer in one sentence: A homeowner — often newly moved in or inspired by a Houzz or Instagram reference — who wants their yard transformed into something beautiful, fears being ghosted after paying a deposit, and defaults to photos and peer referrals to choose a contractor.

The student's client in one sentence: A landscaping contractor running 2-6 crews, doing $400K-$2M in annual revenue across maintenance and design/installation work, with inconsistent inbound leads and no systematic way to convert website visitors into yard assessment appointments.

Top 5 insights:

1. The deposit ghost is the defining fear in this niche. Three of four negative reviews describe a contractor who took a deposit and disappeared. No competing agency or contractor website addresses this fear directly. A website that states "your deposit is protected by a written contract — here is what that means" is immediately differentiated in a niche where this failure mode is well-known among homeowners.

2. "Bring my vision to life" is the homeowner's actual purchase language. Reviews use this phrase directly and consistently. The landscaping website that speaks to the homeowner's imagination — with specific before/after galleries, named project types, and clear style references — converts at a higher rate than one that lists services.

3. Referral culture means the website converts, not generates. Landscaping is primarily word-of-mouth driven. The website is the destination that confirms the referral, not the primary discovery tool. This means the website's job is confirmation and trust-building, not cold inbound. Social proof quality matters more than SEO volume in the short term.

4. The lawn care and landscape design buyer are not the same person. A homeowner scheduling weekly mowing makes a $50-$80 decision with low emotional investment. A homeowner commissioning a $20,000 backyard transformation has a 3-month research and comparison process. One website trying to speak to both without differentiating the journey converts neither well.

5. Visual portfolio specificity is the #1 conversion driver, and no agency produces it well. Most competitor landscaping sites show generic photo grids without project type labels, cost ranges, or homeowner context. A filterable, labeled before/after gallery with project descriptions is the highest-value, lowest-cost differentiator the student can build into every client site.

Top 3 risks if we pick this niche:

- Landscaping is highly visual; without quality job photos from the client, the website cannot perform. Onboarding the client's existing photo library is a prerequisite; clients who have not been documenting their work are a risk.
- The niche splits into two buyer personas (maintenance vs design); serving both well requires two distinct conversion flows, adding complexity to the factory template.
- Project-based revenue is lumpy and seasonal; contractors may pause website services between seasons if not on a retainer.

Top 3 reasons to pick this niche:

- The deposit protection gap is unaddressed by every competitor and is the single highest-fear issue in the niche. A student who builds this into every site has an immediate differentiator.
- Average project tickets of $12,000-$20,000 mean one extra closed project per month more than covers the website investment; the ROI story is straightforward.
- The niche is highly fragmented with no dominant national agency; most landscaping contractors are working with generalist agencies or no agency at all, which means the entry bar is low and the quality bar on existing sites is correspondingly low.

Money math headline: "A landscaping contractor who closes one extra backyard project per month from their own website covers the site investment in the first week and that homeowner typically becomes a maintenance customer for three more years."
