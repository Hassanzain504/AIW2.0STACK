The pills, and what
the name actually means

One page reworked, so the change can be judged before it is applied to the other fifty six. Two things move together here: the pill row that reads as generated, and a palette that takes the name and the veteran status seriously without putting the site in camouflage.

THE NAME

Black Ops means discretion, not combat

Read literally, the name points at tactical gear, camouflage and night vision green. That is the obvious route and it is the wrong one. It would make the hoarding page feel like an intervention and the trauma page feel like a game, and both of those pages are written to do the opposite.

But there is a second reading, and it is already written into every specialty page in the decks: unmarked vehicles, no company signage on site, neighbours see a plain van, technicians arrive in plain clothing and change on site.

That is the identity worth owning

The name is a promise about being invisible. Not about force. A family calling at 2am wants exactly that, and so does a property manager in a shared building who does not want the whole lobby to know.

None of the eight sites in the teardown owns discretion visually. Bio-One mentions it in copy. Nobody builds an identity on it. It is uncontested, it is true, and it comes straight from the service rather than from the name being decorated.

The veteran half works the same way. Service and documentation, not combat. The visual language of that is insignia and brass, not field camouflage.

The rule that keeps this out of cliché territory

A near-black ground with one warm accent is a known generated-page look, and the frontend-design skill names it. This avoids it on two counts, and both are rules rather than taste.

Black is punctuation, not the ground. It appears in the header, the spec strip and the footer. The page itself is warm off-white. The generated version makes the whole page black and lets one accent glow on it.

Brass is a metal, not a highlight. Desaturated and mid-value, so it reads as an insignia rather than as a neon pop. It never glows and it never gradients.

All twelve contrast pairings pass WCAG AA, computed not estimated. The tightest is brass on field black at 4.61, which is the spec strip itself, so that is the one that mattered most.

WHY

Four kinds of thing, three destinations

The middot was the symptom. The actual fault is that a checkable federal registration and an adjective were given the same visual weight and the same shape. Once they are separated, the middot has nothing left to join.

Facts, to the spec strip

Anything a stranger can verify without asking. Rockville MD 20852, PMPPFTB7CCX4, 561720, the licence number.

Label in plain words, value in mono. The mono is carrying actual codes here, which is the one case where it is information rather than decoration.

Promises, to the sentence

Answered 24 hours, unmarked vehicles, we bill your insurer, written price first.

These need their context. Unmarked vehicles as a lone tag means nothing; as a sentence it says the neighbours will not know. The 24 hour claim also gets its pair back, the phone and the on-site window stated together as batch two requires.

Credentials, to the trust bar

GBAC, ISSA, insurance, bonding. Each one keeps its own line with room, and each carries a number underneath once the client supplies it.

GBAC trained is decoration. GBAC trained, cert 4471 is information, and it is the number that makes it unfakeable.

The side effect worth noticing

The spec strip is the thing no competitor in this market has. Not one of the eight sites in the teardown publishes a UEI or a NAICS code anywhere, let alone on the home page. For a federal or county buyer it answers the qualifying question before they scroll.

So removing an AI tell also produced the strongest differentiator on the page. That is usually how it goes: the generated pattern was occupying space that a real fact wanted.

Two things I have not solved here

Type is unresolved on purpose. The mock uses the same faces as these working documents, which are not the site's faces. The font roster comes from the niche playbook that Module 2D writes, and picking now means picking twice. Type carries a lot of the natural-versus-generated feeling, so this is a real gap, not a detail.

The hero still has no photograph. No palette and no strip fixes that. A real photograph of Amilcar and a real job is worth more than everything on this page combined, and it is the one thing only the client can supply.
