# Website Review
## Bethany Associates

Prepared 5 August 2026

---

## Before anything else

You have built something real. Twenty five years in business. A 4.9 rating. Over 15,000 customers. Reviews on your site from people who have used you for a decade.

That is the hard part, and it is done.

This review is about the gap between that reputation and what your website does with it. Most of what follows is not about design. Your site looks fine. It is about the specific points where someone who wanted to hire you does not, and where people searching for your service never find you in the first place.

I looked at your homepage, your contact page, your team page, five of your service pages, and all of your site's sitemaps. Everything below is something I measured, not an opinion.

---

## The short version

There are four things costing you real money.

1. **There is no way to ask for a price on your homepage.**
2. **Your team page has nine names and no photographs.**
3. **You have around 539 location pages, and they are near-identical copies of each other.**
4. **Your homepage takes about 1.25 MB to deliver 691 words.**

Each one is explained below in plain terms.

---

## 1. There is nowhere to ask for a quote

I opened your homepage and scrolled all the way to the bottom. There is no form. Not one.

To request a quote, a visitor has to notice a button, click it, wait for a second page to load, then wait for the form itself to load in from another company's server, and only then start typing.

When they get there, the form asks for:

- First name
- Last name
- Email
- Phone
- Which services they want
- Street address
- City
- State
- Country
- Postal code
- Notes about the project
- Two agreements about text messages

Twelve things, on a phone, before they can hit send.

And one detail matters more than all the rest. **Email is required. Phone number is optional.**

A homeowner who wants their windows cleaned wants you to ring them. Right now your form treats their phone number as the part they can skip.

**What this should be:** a short form on the homepage itself, visible without scrolling. Name, phone, postcode. Three boxes. Everything else gets asked on the call, where you are going to ask it anyway.

---

## 2. Your team page shows no faces

You have a page called Meet the Team. It lists nine people with their job titles. President, Vice President, Office Manager, Operations Manager, and your sales team.

There are no photographs of any of them.

Think about what your customer is actually deciding. They are deciding whether to let people they have never met put ladders against their house and walk around their property, often while they are at work.

A page called Meet the Team that shows no one is a harder read than no page at all, because they clicked it expecting faces.

There is a second half to this. I searched your entire homepage for the word **insured**. It does not appear. Not once. The same for **licensed** and **bonded**.

Your crews work at height. On ladders, on roofs, on gutters. "What if they fall, or break something" is the loudest question in a nervous homeowner's head, and your homepage never answers it.

**What this should be:** nine photographs, taken on a phone, one sentence each. And the words "fully insured" near your phone number at the top of every page. This is the cheapest change on this entire list and probably the most valuable.

## 3. Your location pages are copies of each other

This is the biggest finding, and it needs a moment of explanation.

Your website publishes location pages in four separate batches:

| Type of page | How many |
|---|---|
| `/window-cleaning/{town}` | 61 |
| `/window-washing/{town}` | 60 |
| `/window-cleaning-nj/{town}` | **356** |
| `/christmas-lights/{town}` | 62 |

That is roughly **539 location pages** for a business run out of one office in Hammonton.

I took two of them and compared them word by word. Your Hammonton window cleaning page, and your Mount Laurel window cleaning page.

**They are 96.6% identical.**

Here is the opening of each:

> **Hammonton page:** "Are you looking for expert window cleaning in **Hammonton, NJ**? Bethany Associates provides trusted window cleaning services for residential and commercial properties. With over 25 years of experience..."

> **Mount Laurel page:** "Are you looking for expert window cleaning in **Mount Laurel, NJ**? Bethany Associates provides trusted window cleaning services for residential and commercial properties. With over 25 years of experience..."

The same sentence with the town swapped. Across hundreds of pages.

Google is very good at recognising this pattern, and it does not reward it. Worse, it can hold back the pages that genuinely deserve to rank, because the site as a whole starts to look automated rather than local.

### Two more things inside this

**You compete against yourself.** For someone searching "window cleaning Hammonton NJ", you have three separate pages on your own site, each one telling Google it is the right one:

- `/window-cleaning/hammonton-nj`
- `/window-washing/hammonton-nj`
- `/hammonton-nj/window-cleaning`

Window cleaning and window washing are the same service. Two of those pages are the same thing with a different word. Instead of one strong page, you have three weak ones splitting the same ground.

**You have pages for towns you do not serve.** That batch of 356 covers the whole state, including Morristown, Mahwah, Montvale and Lodi. Those are all in North Jersey, well over a hundred miles from Hammonton. There is also a page for Lower Makefield, which is in Pennsylvania, filed among your New Jersey pages.

**What this should be:** twenty to thirty genuine pages for the towns you actually serve, each with real local content, a real job you did there, and a photo from that job. The rest redirected properly so nothing is lost.

## 4. Your site is heavy

Some numbers from your homepage:

| | |
|---|---|
| Total page weight | about **1.25 MB** |
| Of that, code in the page itself | 561 KB |
| Of that, styling code alone | **405 KB** |
| Separate script files loading | **48** |
| Actual words on the page | **691** |

That bottom line is the one worth sitting with. **1.25 megabytes to deliver 691 words**, which is about two sides of A4.

Most of that weight is 405 KB of styling code pasted directly into the top of every page. A browser cannot display anything until it has read through all of it. And because it is pasted in rather than saved as a separate file, your visitor downloads all 405 KB again on the next page they visit, and the one after that.

Two smaller things add to it. Your before and after photographs, which are the most persuasive images on your whole site, were saved in a format meant for logos rather than photographs, which makes them about five times larger than they need to be. And several images do not tell the browser what size they are, which is why the page jumps around as it loads on a phone.

## 5. Smaller items worth knowing about

**The line Google shows for you does not have your name in it.**
Your homepage headline in search results currently reads "Exterior Cleaning & Pressure Washing | Professional Services". No business name. No Hammonton. No New Jersey. Interestingly, a much better version already exists in your site's code, it is just being used for Facebook sharing instead of Google.

**Google cannot see your 4.9 rating.**
Your rating and your 15,000 customers are displayed on the page as text and graphics. There is no code telling Google what they mean. That is why you have no star rating showing next to your listing in search results. You have earned the stars. They are just not readable.

**You have two phone numbers and two addresses.**
Your site shows an 888 number nearly everywhere, and your local 609 number appears once, on your contact page, marked "office". Your footer shows a PO Box while the code behind your site and your map both show 3001 South White Horse Pike.

Google cross-checks all of this against your Google Business Profile and your directory listings. When it does not line up, local ranking suffers. There is also a plainer point here: people in South Jersey recognise a 609 number as one of their own. An 888 number reads as a call centre somewhere else.

**Your hours say you are closed all weekend.**
Monday to Friday, closing at 4:30pm. Your customers are at work then. They are at home on Saturday morning, looking at their windows, deciding to do something. On Saturday morning your site offers them a phone number to a closed office and no form to fill in.

**Your reviews are signed with initials.**
"F.B." and "G.W." Real reviews signed with initials tend to read as invented, even when they are not. A first name and a town would fix that.

**Your buttons ask for nothing.**
Eight buttons on your homepage say "Learn More". That phrase makes no promise and asks for nothing.

**No prices anywhere.**
There is not a single figure on the site. Unknown cost is the most common reason a homeowner puts off enquiring. A simple "from $X" gives serious customers the confidence to get in touch, and quietly filters out the ones who were never going to book.

## What this is costing you

I do not have access to your traffic figures, so I cannot give you a real number. What I can do is show you the shape of it, and you can put your own figures in.

| | |
|---|---|
| Visitors to your site each month | your number |
| How many would enquire on a site with a form on the homepage | roughly 5 in 100 |
| How many enquire when they must click through to a 12-field form | typically less than half of that |
| Of those enquiries, how many book | your number |
| What one customer is worth per visit | your number |
| **How many times a year that customer comes back** | **this is the part that matters** |

That last line is why this adds up. Window cleaning is not a one-off. A customer you lose on a Tuesday because they could not find your form is not one missed job. It is every clean that customer would have booked for the next several years, plus everyone they would have recommended you to.

Every finding above is a small leak. Individually none of them look serious. Together they are the difference between a website that displays your business and a website that grows it.

---

## Where I would start

Not everything here is equal. If you did nothing else, these four would move the needle most, and none of them is a big project:

1. **A three-field quote form on your homepage,** with phone as the required field rather than email.
2. **Photographs of your team, and the words "fully insured"** near your phone number.
3. **One phone number and one address** used consistently everywhere.
4. **Your business name and your town in your Google search headline.**

Those four are days of work, not months.

The location pages and the site's weight are bigger jobs. They are worth doing, but they are a separate conversation and I would not start there.

Everything in this review was measured directly from your live website on 5 August 2026. The page weights come from the files your server actually sent. The 96.6% figure comes from comparing the visible text of two of your pages word by word. The page counts come from your own sitemaps.
