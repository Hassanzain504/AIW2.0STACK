Black Ops Cleaning
Review engine
Workstream 2

From zero reviews
to a system

Black Ops has zero reviews on every platform. Its Rockville competitors have hundreds. This is the routine that closes that gap: a walkthrough, a QR code the technician shows at handover, a capture page that takes name, mobile and email, and a two message follow up that runs on both channels and then stops.

01

Two rules the whole system is built on

Everything below follows from these. They are why the capture page has no rating question on it.

Rule one, never branch on a rating

Do not ask "how did we do, 1 to 5" and send high scorers to Google while low scorers go somewhere private. That is review gating. Google bans it and removes all of a business's reviews when it catches it, not just the gated ones. The FTC rule on consumer reviews has covered review suppression since 21 October 2024, with civil penalties around $51,744 per violation.

Black Ops is SAM.gov registered and bidding for federal work, so an FTC action against them is a contracting problem, not just a marketing one.

Rule two, fix it on site, then ask everyone the same way

The technician walks the finished job with the customer and asks whether anything is not right. Anything raised gets fixed there. That twenty second habit prevents more bad reviews than any filtering ever could, because it catches the problem while the crew is still standing in the room.

Then the ask goes out, to everyone, identically. The capture page offers a public review and a private line to the owner side by side, both equally prominent, and the customer picks. Most unhappy customers choose the private one on their own, which is the outcome you wanted. The difference is that they chose it.

02

The target number

Not a round number. The actual count needed to compete in the map pack for each keyword and town.

Reviews do two separate jobs. First, review count and how regularly new ones arrive are among the strongest factors in whether Google shows a business in the map block at all. Second, they decide whether anyone calls: three businesses appear in that block and a facility manager compares three star ratings in about four seconds. At zero reviews you lose that comparison every time, even when you rank.

Recency beats total

A new company with thirty real reviews arriving steadily beats a ten year old company with a half built profile and twelve. This is why the routine matters more than any push, and why the target is a monthly rate rather than a finish line.

03

The QR code and the capture page

One QR code, printed once, that never changes. It points at a page on the client's own site, which means the destination can be edited without reprinting anything.

Why the QR points at your own page, not straight at Google

  • You capture the contact. A QR straight to Google gets you a review or nothing, and you never learn who the customer was. Through your own page you get name, email and phone, which is what makes follow up possible at all.
  • The destination stays editable. Stickers on a van and blocks on invoices are printed once. If the Google link changes, or you add BBB later, you edit the page rather than reprint the van.
  • You can measure it. Scans, form completions and click-throughs to Google are all trackable. A direct QR to Google is a black box.

Print the QR once, and only once

Test every printed QR from three phones before anything goes to a printer. A dead QR on a van panel is expensive, and nobody reports it, they just do not scan.

Keep at least 2cm of clear space around it and print it at 2.5cm square or larger. Below that, older phone cameras start failing in poor light, which is most of a technician's working day.

Blocked until the Google profile verifies

The capture page can be built now, but the public review button has nowhere to point until the Google Business Profile is verified. That is the one to three week item in the citations runbook. Submit day one.

Suppression rules, which matter more than the scripts

  • Clicked either link, stop the sequence. Nothing is more irritating than being chased for something you already did.
  • Replied privately, stop the sequence and route it to Amilcar the same day. Someone who has just told you a problem must not receive a review request two days later.
  • Replied STOP or unsubscribed, stop everything, across both channels, not just the one they used.
  • No consent ticked, no sequence. The job still closes normally, they just do not get the follow up.
  • Never contact the same customer about a second job until at least ninety days have passed.

The specialty exception

On trauma, unattended death and crime scene work, the QR is not produced and the sequence does not run. Many families will not want to be publicly connected to the job, and an automated review request two days after a death is the worst message this business could send.

The rule for the crew: on those jobs, the paperwork email goes out with no review ask in it. If the family has been open and warm throughout, the technician may ask in person, once, gently, and never by text or automation. If there is any doubt, do not ask. That work builds through referrals from funeral directors, property managers and estate solicitors instead, which is slower and worth more.

05

The commercial track, which only Amilcar can do

Business to business reviews do not come from an automation. They come from a phone call. This is the part of the engagement that cannot be delegated.

A janitorial contract has no moment of completion. Nobody feels a rush of gratitude on a Tuesday because the bins were emptied again. So the text sequence above does nothing here, and the first twenty commercial reviews have to come from the owner personally calling clients he has already served.

Owner callAmilcar, to an existing client
Hi [name], it's Amilcar from Black Ops. Nothing wrong, this is not a problem call. Two things. First, is there anything about how we are servicing your building that you would change? I would rather hear it from you than assume. [Listen. Write it down. Fix whatever comes up, and tell them when it is fixed.] Second, a favour. We are building up our Google profile and reviews from facility managers carry more weight than anything I could write myself. If you would be willing to leave a couple of lines, I will text you the link now. And if you would rather not, that is genuinely fine and it changes nothing.
The service question comes first and it is real. It surfaces problems before they become churn, and it means the ask is not the only reason for the call.
RuleWhy
Five calls a week, not twenty in one dayReviews that arrive in a burst read as bought, and Google filters them
Call, do not emailA facility manager ignores the email and takes the call
Text the link during the callWhile you are still on the phone and it is top of mind

Replies, to every review

Within 24 hours, naming the service and the town, because those replies are indexed too. Templates are starting points, not scripts to paste verbatim.

Five starReply within 24 hours
Thank you [name]. It was a pleasure working with you on the [service] at your [property type] in [town]. If anything comes up down the line, you know where we are. Amilcar, Black Ops Cleaning
Naming the service and the town in the reply is a free relevance signal on a page Google already reads. Vary the wording, never paste the same reply twice.
Three starReply within 24 hours
Thank you for taking the time, [name], and for being straight with me about [the specific issue]. You are right that [acknowledge it plainly]. Here is what we have changed since: [the actual change]. If you would give us another go on the [service], I will handle the scheduling personally. Either way, thank you for telling us. Amilcar, Black Ops Cleaning
A three star with a specific complaint is the most useful review the business will get. Answer the complaint, not the rating.
One starReply within 24 hours
[Name], I am sorry. This is not the standard we hold ourselves to and I am not going to argue with you in public about it. I would like to understand what happened and put it right. My direct number is [owner number] and I will pick up. Amilcar, owner, Black Ops Cleaning
Short, no defensiveness, no version of events, and it moves the conversation off a public page. Every prospective customer who reads that thread is judging the reply, not the review.

Three things never to do in a reply

Never dispute the facts in public. Even when you are right, you look worse than the complaint did.

Never mention anything about the job that identifies the customer, particularly on trauma, biohazard or hoarding work. That is a privacy failure and on a health related job it can be a serious one.

Never threaten legal action to get a review removed. Unfounded legal threats to suppress a review are specifically prohibited under the FTC rule.

07

Pace, channels and what to measure

Pace

  • Five to ten a month reads as real. Forty in one week reads as bought, and Google filters it.
  • Steady beats sudden, permanently. Recency is a ranking factor, so a business that stops for three months goes backwards even if the total is high.
  • Never stop once the target is hit. The target is a rate, not a finish line.

Channels, in order

ChannelSolicitNotes
Google Business ProfileYesThe one that decides map pack placement. Everything else is secondary.
FacebookYesOnly after the two duplicate pages are merged. Reviews split across duplicates are wasted.
BBBYesCarries weight with commercial and government buyers specifically.
YelpNeverYelp filters solicited reviews aggressively and it can affect the listing. Claim it, keep it accurate, leave it alone.
Industry directoriesWhere offeredISSA, GBAC and the veteran directories, once those listings exist.

What to measure, monthly

  • New reviews per platform, against the target rate
  • Ask rate. Jobs completed versus asks sent. If that gap is wide the routine is not running, whatever the review count says
  • Scan rate. Jobs completed versus QR scans. Under half means the technicians are skipping step 2, and that is a coaching problem not a copy problem
  • Capture rate. Scans versus completed forms. Under seventy percent means the form is asking too much or the consent line is scaring people
  • Conversion. Asks sent versus reviews received. Under ten percent means the ask is landing wrong or landing late
  • Reply time. Every review answered inside 24 hours, no exceptions
  • Map pack position for the target keywords, which is the number all of this actually moves
