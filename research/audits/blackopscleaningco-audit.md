## 1. Performance. Homepage weighs 7.6 MB (Critical)

Measured asset weight on the homepage:

| Asset | Size | Format |
|---|---|---|
| Commerical-Buildings-1024x683.png | 1297 KB | PNG |
| Website-Model-1-1.png | 1289 KB | PNG |
| Crime-Scene-3-1024x683.png | 1285 KB | PNG |
| Capitol-Hill-3-1024x681.png | 1194 KB | PNG |
| advancer-sw4000tm-rider-sweeper-49_1200x1200-copy.png | 1066 KB | PNG |
| Hospital-Building-1-1-1024x681.png | 913 KB | PNG |

Total page weight approximately 7.6 MB across 17 measured assets.

The server itself is fine. TTFB measured at 0.28s, LiteSpeed cache is hitting
(`x-litespeed-cache: hit`). This is purely an image problem, which makes it a cheap fix.

Root cause: photographs saved as PNG instead of WebP or AVIF. PNG is a lossless format meant
for logos and screenshots. A photograph as PNG is roughly 10x larger than the same image as
WebP at visually identical quality.

Expected result of fixing: 7.6 MB drops to roughly 600 to 900 KB. On a phone on 4G this is the
difference between a hero image appearing in 1 second versus 8 seconds. LCP is a confirmed
ranking signal and a much larger conversion signal.

Fix:
- Convert every photograph to WebP with AVIF fallback, quality 75 to 82
- Resize to the actual display dimensions, do not serve 1200px into a 400px slot
- Enable LiteSpeed Cache image optimisation (already installed on this host, just not configured)
- Preload the hero LCP image, lazy load everything below the fold
- Note the filename typo: `Commerical-Buildings` should be `Commercial-Buildings`. Rename on
  re-upload with a descriptive keyword-relevant filename.

## 2. Structured data. No LocalBusiness schema (Critical)

The homepage carries exactly one JSON-LD block, generated by Yoast defaults:

```
WebPage, ImageObject, BreadcrumbList, WebSite, Organization
```

The `Organization` node has `sameAs` pointing at Facebook and Instagram, and nothing else.
Missing entirely:

- `LocalBusiness` (or the more specific `CleaningService` type)
- `telephone`
- `address` / `PostalAddress`
- `areaServed` for DC, Maryland, Virginia and the individual counties
- `geo` coordinates
- `openingHoursSpecification`
- `Service` schema on the services page
- `FAQPage` (there is no FAQ content on the site at all)
- `AggregateRating` (nothing to mark up yet, see issue 3)

For a service area business this is one of the cheapest wins available. Structured data is also
how AI answer engines identify and cite a business, which matters more every quarter.

## 3. Reviews. Effectively zero social proof (Critical)

Verified off-site:

- Angi: listed, "new business, no reviews yet"
- Facebook: "Not yet rated (0 Reviews)"
- HomeAdvisor: listed
- Rockville Chamber of Commerce: listed (this is a real local citation, good)
- The website itself contains zero testimonials, zero review widgets, zero named clients

Google Business Profile review count could not be confirmed from outside and needs dashboard
access, but given every other platform is at zero it is very likely low or zero.

This is the single biggest blocker to map pack ranking and the single biggest conversion killer.
A facility manager choosing a cleaning vendor for a hospital or a government building will not
be the first reviewer. Someone has to go first.

Starting point: every past and current client, contacted personally by the founder, one at a
time, over four to six weeks. Not a bulk email. B2B review generation is a phone call, not
an automation.

## 4. Site architecture. Four pages total (Critical)

Full sitemap:

```
/
/contact/
/services/
/about/
```

That is the entire site. Consequences:

- Every service competes for ranking on one shared `/services/` page. Nothing can rank for
  "biohazard cleanup Bethesda" or "medical facility cleaning Silver Spring" because no page
  targets those.
- No location pages, so ranking is capped to roughly the proximity radius of the Rockville pin.
  The DMV is enormous. Arlington, Alexandria, Bethesda, Silver Spring, Gaithersburg, Columbia,
  DC proper are all effectively unreachable organically right now.
- No blog, so there is no route to informational or comparison queries, and nothing for AI
  answer engines to cite.

Pages the business should have, ordered by likely revenue:

Service pages (one each):
1. Commercial janitorial / office cleaning
2. Medical and healthcare facility cleaning
3. Government and federal facility cleaning
4. Biohazard remediation
5. Crime scene and trauma cleanup
6. Sewage backup and water damage decontamination
7. Carpet extraction
8. Hard floor care and stripping / waxing
9. Window cleaning
10. Pressure washing
11. Disinfection and infection control
12. Post-construction cleaning
13. Emergency response cleaning

Location pages (one each, only where they will genuinely travel):
Rockville, Bethesda, Silver Spring, Gaithersburg, Washington DC, Arlington VA,
Alexandria VA, Fairfax VA, Columbia MD, Frederick MD

That is a realistic 20 to 25 page build, shipped at 2 to 3 pages a week over two to three months.
Do not bulk generate them. Each location page needs a real job reference, a real photo and a
real local detail or it will be treated as a doorway page.

## 5. Compliance. CDC, EPA and OSHA logos on the site (Critical, non-SEO)

The homepage displays:

```
/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/CDC-Logo-300x169.png
/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/EPA-Logo-300x169.png
/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/OSHA-Logo-300x169.png
```

These are US federal agency logos. CDC, EPA and OSHA all restrict use of their seals and logos,
and displaying them next to a company's certifications implies endorsement or affiliation that
does not exist. This is a legal exposure, not an SEO issue, and it is worse than usual here
because the company is actively pursuing federal contracting work, where an implied-endorsement
problem is exactly the kind of thing that disqualifies a bidder.

Remove them. Replace with accurate text claims, which are just as persuasive and are true:

- "Follows CDC and EPA guidance for disinfection protocols"
- "OSHA bloodborne pathogen compliant"
- "EPA List N registered disinfectants"

Keep the genuine certification logos: ISSA, GBAC (both verified on the page), and the Greater
Rockville Chamber of Commerce membership badge. Those are real memberships and are earned.

## 6. Missing H1 tags (High)

| Page | H1 count | H2 count |
|---|---|---|
| Homepage | 1 | 7 |
| /services/ | 0 | 4 |
| /about/ | 0 | 8 |
| /contact/ | 0 | 4 |

Three of four pages have no H1 at all. This is a classic Elementor problem: the heading widget
defaults to H2 and nobody changes it. Every page needs exactly one H1 that states what the page
is about in the language a buyer would use.

Homepage H1 is currently "Precision Cleaning, Every Time". That is a tagline, not an H1.
It contains no service and no location. Something like
"Commercial Cleaning and Biohazard Remediation in the DMV" does the same brand job while
actually telling Google what the page is.

## 7. Title tag cannibalisation (High)

```
Homepage:  Professional Cleaning Services in DMV | Black Ops Cleaning
/services/: Professional Cleaning Services DMV | Black Ops Cleaning Co.
```

These are near-identical. Google has to guess which one to rank for the core query, and the
usual outcome is that it flips between them and neither builds authority. Differentiate:

- Homepage: brand and primary positioning, for example
  "Veteran-Owned Commercial Cleaning in the DMV | Black Ops Cleaning Co."
- Services: a hub page, for example
  "Commercial, Medical & Biohazard Cleaning Services | DMV"

## 8. Meta descriptions contradict the positioning (High)

```
Homepage:  "...veteran-owned, precision cleaning services for homes and businesses..."
/services/: "...eco-friendly residential, commercial, and specialized cleaning..."
```

But the actual page content, the H2s ("Facility Cleaning & Specialty Cleanup Services"), the
certifications (ISSA CMI, GBAC), the imagery (hospital, Capitol Hill, commercial buildings,
crime scene) and the founder's own positioning are all commercial, medical and government.
Nothing on the site supports a residential offering.

Two problems. It drags in residential leads the business does not want and cannot serve
profitably, and it confuses Google about what the entity actually is. Pick one. Based on
everything else on the site, the answer is commercial, medical, government and specialty.

Also a literal typo in the homepage meta description: "Black Ops Cleaning. offers" has a stray
full stop mid-sentence.

## 9. Image alt text (High)

32 `<img>` tags on the homepage. 22 of them have `alt=""`. None are missing the attribute
entirely, so this is Elementor writing empty alts rather than broken markup, but the effect is
the same: no accessibility, no image search visibility, and a wasted relevance signal on a page
that is mostly images.

Write real alt text describing what is in the photo, including the service and location where
it is genuinely true.

## 10. Duplicate Facebook pages (High)

Two distinct Facebook URLs exist for this business:

```
https://www.facebook.com/people/Black-Ops-Cleaning-Co/61565688962753/   (linked in site schema)
https://www.facebook.com/BlackOpsCleaningCompany/                        (found in search)
```

Duplicates split reviews, split followers, and weaken the entity signal. Merge them in Facebook
settings, keep the one with more history, and update the `sameAs` in schema to match.

Run the same duplicate check on Google Business Profile, Bing Places, Yelp and Apple Business
Connect. Duplicate GBP listings are one of the most common causes of a business being invisible
in the map pack and one of the easiest things to miss.

## 11. No address and no map (Medium)

No street address appears anywhere in the crawled HTML. No Google Maps embed on any page,
including the contact page.

For a service area business, hiding the street address on Google Business Profile is correct.
But the website should still clearly state the city and state, list the service area explicitly,
and embed a map of the coverage area. Right now a buyer in Arlington has no way to confirm this
company covers them, and Google has no on-page confirmation of location beyond nine mentions of
"Rockville".

Whatever address is used on the GBP must match whatever appears on the website, the Chamber
listing, Angi, HomeAdvisor and every other citation, character for character.

## 12. Contact form (Medium)

The contact page has one Elementor form with these fields:

```
name, email, message, field_24a54c2, field_bc0a951
```

Two fields are unnamed in the markup, which usually means a default label was never set. There
is no clearly identifiable phone field, and no field capturing service type, facility type,
square footage or building count. For commercial cleaning, those qualifying fields are the
difference between a usable quote request and an email that takes three rounds to qualify.

Add: phone (required), service needed (dropdown), facility type, approximate square footage,
cleaning frequency, and preferred contact time.

Also confirm where submissions actually go and that they are not landing in a spam folder.
This is a leak worth testing on day one with a live submission.

## 13. Photography (Medium)

`smiling-cleaner-vacuuming-floor-in-office-near-mul-968JLYQa.png` is a stock photo, identifiable
from the stock library filename pattern. Several other images look like stock or supplier
product shots (the Advancer sweeper image is a manufacturer product photo).

Commercial buyers are unusually good at spotting stock photography and it costs trust
immediately. The fix is free: the crew photographs every job. Before and after, the truck, the
equipment, the team in uniform. Build it into the job close-out process.

Separately, the "Crime-Scene" image sits on the homepage alongside commercial janitorial
content. A facility manager shopping for weekly office cleaning does not need to see that first.
Move trauma and biohazard imagery onto its own dedicated service page, where it belongs and
where it will convert far better.

## 14. Hours versus offer (Medium)

The site advertises "emergency cleaning response" and "sewage backup decontamination" while
listing hours as Monday to Friday, 9:00 AM to 5:00 PM, with after-hours "by appointment".

Sewage backups and biohazard incidents are by definition after hours and on weekends. A buyer
in that situation calls the company that answers. Either commit to a 24/7 emergency line and
say so prominently, or stop advertising emergency response. The current state loses the highest
margin calls in the entire service list.

## 15. Measurement (Medium)

No call tracking number is in use. The same number appears on the site and, presumably, on the
GBP. There is no way to attribute a phone call to organic, map pack, paid or offline.

Before any work starts: install call tracking with dynamic number insertion, set GA4 events for
call clicks and form submissions, and confirm Search Console is verified. Without this, month
three becomes an argument about whether anything worked.

## 16. The differentiators are completely unmonetised (High, and the actual opportunity)

Everything above is cleanup. This is the growth item.

This business holds four genuinely uncommon assets and monetises none of them:

**Veteran-owned, with formal certification in progress.** The site says they are "pursuing
formal veteran-owned business certification". In the DMV, sitting on top of the federal
government, SDVOSB or VOSB certification is not a trust badge, it is a procurement channel.
Set-aside contracts are restricted to certified firms. This deserves its own page targeting
government facility contracting, plus SAM.gov registration, plus the certification finished
as a priority. Nobody else in their competitive set can copy this.

**GBAC and ISSA CMI certification.** Genuine, verifiable, and rare among small janitorial
firms. Should anchor a page on infection control and healthcare facility cleaning, which is
higher margin than office janitorial and has far fewer credible bidders.

**Biohazard, trauma and crime scene cleanup.** Very few competitors offer this. Very high
margin. Extremely high commercial intent when someone searches for it. Currently it is one
line item on a shared services page and a stock image on the homepage. It should be a full
page cluster with its own location variants.

**Sewage backup and emergency decontamination.** Urgent, high value, low competition, and
completely undermined by the 9 to 5 hours problem in issue 14.

The current site is positioned as a generic cleaning company that also does some other things.
The business is actually a specialty remediation and certified facility cleaning firm, owned by
a veteran, in the highest concentration of federal facilities in the country. Those are
different businesses and they command different prices.

## Recommended order of work

**Week 1. Access and stop the bleeding.**
GBP, Search Console, GA4, hosting and DNS access. Baseline geogrid scan and screenshots.
Install call tracking. Remove the CDC, EPA and OSHA logos. Compress every image.
Fix the H1s, titles and meta descriptions. Add LocalBusiness schema.

**Weeks 2 to 4. Google Business Profile and reviews.**
Full GBP audit and rebuild: primary category, service area, all services with descriptions,
Products section, weekly posts, Q&A seeding, real photo uploads. Duplicate listing sweep.
Start the review programme: founder calls every past client personally.

**Weeks 3 to 12. Build the site out.**
Two to three real pages a week. Highest margin services first: biohazard, medical facility,
government facility, emergency response. Then location pages for the highest value cities.
Add real photography as jobs complete.

**Month 2 onward. Citations, links and authority.**
NAP cleanup across every platform. Chamber of Commerce (already a member, get the link).
Veteran business directories. Local business associations. Property management and facility
management partnerships. SDVOSB certification and SAM.gov registration.

**Ongoing. Reporting.**
Monthly one-pager: geogrid movement, map pack keyword count, GBP calls and direction requests,
Search Console clicks, reviews added, leads by source, quotes and jobs won.

## Expectation setting for the client

- Image and technical fixes show in Core Web Vitals within days, in rankings within weeks
- GBP work typically moves the map pack in 2 to 6 weeks
- New service and location pages take 3 to 6 months to mature
- Reviews are the long pole and depend entirely on the client doing the asking
- The federal and SDVOSB angle is a 6 to 12 month play with the largest payoff

## Needs dashboard access to complete

- Google Business Profile: verification status, primary category, review count and velocity,
  service area setup, duplicate or suspended listings, insight trends
- Google Search Console: index coverage, current queries, manual actions
- Google Analytics 4: current traffic, sources, conversion tracking status
- Current lead volume, sources, close rate and average job value
- Any previous agency work, and whether their access is still active
