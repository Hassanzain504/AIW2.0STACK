## Why window cleaning, and why now (the research this is built on)

These are the facts the content leans on. Numbers are pulled from public 2024 to 2026 industry data. Sources are listed at the bottom. Use them, but if you quote a hard number publicly, sanity-check it against your own client results first.

- There are roughly 44,000 window cleaning businesses in the US. The industry does about 2.9 billion dollars a year. The top four companies hold less than 5 percent of the market. This is a fragmented industry with no dominant brand. That is the whole opportunity. Whoever owns the digital shelf in a city wins that city.
- 72 percent of US consumers use Google to research a local business before they call.
- About 85 percent of "window cleaning near me" searches show the Map Pack, and the top 3 map spots take around 42 percent of the clicks. If you are not in the top 3, you are fighting over scraps.
- 57 percent of consumers always or frequently read online reviews before choosing a local business. A profile with 100 plus reviews outranks one with 10, all else equal.
- Northern markets can lose up to 30 percent of revenue in winter. The fixes are commercial contracts, which run year round, and add-on winter work like holiday light installs.
- Commercial storefronts are the stabilizer. One 200 dollar a month storefront is 2,400 dollars a year. Twenty accounts is 48,000 dollars of stable revenue that does not care about the weather.
- Residential runs about 4 to 8 dollars per pane. Commercial runs 50 to 100 dollars an hour, high-rise and specialty 100 plus. Recurring contracts trade a 10 to 20 percent discount for predictable revenue.
- Average profit margin sits between 2 and 10 percent. Most of the gap between a struggling window cleaner and a scaling one is not skill. It is lead flow, route density, and recurring revenue.

The angle for the whole account: window cleaning owners are great at the work and invisible online. Their customers are all searching. The owner who shows up first, has the most reviews, and answers the fastest takes the market. That is a digital problem, and digital problems have digital solutions.
