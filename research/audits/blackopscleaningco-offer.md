# What to Offer Black Ops Cleaning (60-Day Scope)

Internal working doc. Decides what is honestly sellable in 60 days, what is not, how to
structure it, and how to price it.

---

## Start from what 60 days can actually produce

Split every possible promise into three buckets.

### Fully controllable, deliver with confidence
These depend only on the agency doing the work.

- Every technical fix. Homepage from 7.6 MB to under 1 MB. Core Web Vitals green on mobile.
- H1s, title tags, meta descriptions rewritten across the site
- LocalBusiness schema, Service schema, FAQ schema
- CDC, EPA and OSHA logos removed (legal risk closed)
- Google Business Profile rebuilt to 100% complete
- Duplicate Facebook page merged, NAP cleaned across platforms
- 12 to 15 real new pages written and built
- Call tracking and GA4 conversion tracking live
- Citations built
- Review request system built and running
- Reporting in place

### Highly likely, but not guaranteeable
These depend on Google's timing.

- Page 1 for several specialty terms (biohazard, unattended death, hoarding, sewage backup
  in Montgomery County). Thin competition makes this likely inside 8 to 10 weeks, but Google
  decides when.
- Map pack appearances in Rockville for at least one core term
- Search Console impressions climbing sharply

### Cannot be promised in 60 days, and saying so is a selling point
- Top 3 map pack in Washington DC or across the DMV
- A specific number of leads
- A specific revenue figure
- Any ranking outcome that depends on review count, because reviews depend on the client

**The offer sells bucket one, forecasts bucket two, and openly rules out bucket three.**
That third part is what separates this from every other pitch they will hear.

## The offer

### Name it after the outcome, not the service

Not "SEO services". Sell:

> **60 days to a site and profile that can actually rank, and a first position in the
> emergency and biohazard searches nobody local is competing for.**

Two reasons this framing works on this specific client:

1. It is honest. Sixty days buys a foundation, not a market position.
2. It puts the specialty lane first, which is where a real, attributable win can land inside
   the window. One biohazard or unattended death job is worth more than months of janitorial
   revenue. That is the ROI story, and it is achievable.

### Deliverables, fixed and listed in the contract

**Phase 1, Days 1 to 14. Foundation**
- Full access transfer and baseline documentation (screenshots, geogrid, Search Console)
- Call tracking installed with dynamic number insertion, plus GA4 conversion events
- All images compressed and converted to WebP. Homepage under 1 MB.
- Core Web Vitals brought to green on mobile
- H1 added to every page, all title tags and meta descriptions rewritten
- LocalBusiness schema with phone, address, service area, geo, hours
- CDC, EPA and OSHA logos removed and replaced with accurate text claims
- Positioning decision documented and applied sitewide
- Google Business Profile rebuilt: category, service area, all services with descriptions,
  Products section, business description, attributes, hours, 20+ photos
- Duplicate Facebook page merged
- Review request system built: direct link, SMS template, call script, response templates

**Phase 2, Days 15 to 45. The pages**
Fourteen new pages, written and built. Specialty first, because they rank fastest:
1. Biohazard remediation
2. Crime scene and trauma cleanup
3. Unattended death cleanup
4. Sewage backup and decontamination
5. Hoarding cleanup
6. Emergency response cleaning
7. Commercial janitorial and office cleaning
8. Medical and healthcare facility cleaning
9. Government and federal facility cleaning
10. Disinfection and infection control (the GBAC page)
11. Rockville
12. North Bethesda
13. Gaithersburg
14. Bethesda

Each with its own schema, FAQ block, trust bar, pricing guidance and conversion path.

**Phase 3, Days 46 to 60. Authority and proof**
- 30 to 40 citations built, NAP consistent everywhere
- Chamber of Commerce link claimed
- Veteran business directories submitted
- Bing Places and Apple Business Connect claimed
- Weekly GBP posts running on a schedule
- Q&A seeded and answered
- Day 60 report with before and after on every measurable item

### The client's obligations, written into the contract

This is not fine print. It is the clause that protects the agency at day 55.

- Founder personally calls past and current clients for reviews. Target 15 to 25 in 60 days.
- Crew photographs every job. Minimum 5 photos per job, delivered weekly.
- The 24/7 phone decision is made in week 1. Emergency pages do not convert on 9 to 5 hours.
- Positioning question answered in week 1: commercial and specialty, not residential.
- Licence numbers, insurance details and certification documents supplied in week 1.
- Response to drafts within 3 business days.

State plainly: reviews and photos are the two inputs only the client can supply, and results
scale directly with them.

## The guarantee

Guarantee only what is fully controllable. It sounds bold and carries almost no risk.

> **At day 60, if the homepage is not loading in under 2 seconds on mobile, the Google Business
> Profile is not 100% complete, and all 14 pages are not live, the second month is free.**

Every condition is in the agency's own hands. It reads as confidence and costs nothing if the
work is done.

**Optional stronger close, only if it is needed to win the deal:**

> If by day 90 the business is not in the Rockville map pack top 3 for at least one core term,
> and the review and photo obligations above have been met, month 4 is free.

Riskier, and correctly conditional on client input. Hold it in reserve rather than leading
with it.

## Pricing

Currency is USD because the client is US-based.

**Recommended structure: fixed sprint fee, then a monthly retainer.**

| | Range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 60-day sprint | $2,500 to $4,000 | Split 50% up front, 50% at day 30 |
| Monthly retainer from month 3 | $1,200 to $2,000 | 3 month minimum, then rolling |

**First-client pricing, if there is no case study yet:** sprint at $1,500 to $2,000 and retainer
at $1,000, in exchange for a written case study, a video testimonial, and two referrals. Put
those in the contract as consideration. The discount is bought, not given away, and saying so
out loud protects the price for the next client.

**The optional upside, which fits this client unusually well:** a per-job bonus on specialty
work sourced through organic or the map pack. One biohazard or trauma job runs into thousands.
A flat fee per closed specialty job aligns both sides and makes the retainer easy to justify.
Only propose this once call tracking is live, since it is the mechanism that makes it
verifiable. Do not attempt it as a lead-gen-only deal with no tracking history.

**Do not do:** revenue share, twelve month lock-ins with a client who has no reason to trust
the agency yet, or pricing so low that the fourteen pages get rushed. Fourteen properly written
pages is real work.

## What the month 3 retainer covers

Say this during the sale, so the sprint does not look like the whole engagement:

- 4 new pages a month (services, cities, blog)
- GBP management, weekly posts, weekly photo uploads, Q&A
- Review programme management and responses
- Citation and link building
- Technical maintenance
- One monthly report and one call

Frame the sprint as the foundation and the retainer as the compounding, because that is what it
is. Rankings arrive in months 3 to 6, which is exactly when the retainer is running.

## How to open the conversation

Lead with the finding they cannot ignore and did not know about.

The CDC, EPA and OSHA logos on their homepage are a genuine legal exposure, and it is worse for
them than for most because they are pursuing federal contracting, where an implied-endorsement
problem can disqualify a bidder. That is free, specific, verifiable value delivered before any
money is discussed, and it proves the site was actually looked at.

Then the sequence:
1. The logo problem, and that it is fixable in ten minutes
2. The 7.6 MB homepage, with the actual file sizes shown on screen
3. Four pages against a competitor's 151 and another's 618
4. Zero reviews on every platform checked
5. The one that lands hardest: they are sitting on biohazard, GBAC certification and veteran
   ownership in the DMV, and none of it is on the site in a way that can rank or sell

Point five is the pitch. Everything before it is proof that the audit is real.

## What not to offer

- Guaranteed rankings on any timeline
- A promised number of leads inside 60 days
- Anything on "commercial cleaning washington dc" in the first year
- A site rebuild. The platform is fine, TTFB is 0.28s. Offering a rebuild would be selling
  something they do not need, and they will find that out.
- Residential cleaning content
- Social media management, unless they ask. It dilutes the offer and is not where the money is.
- Paid ads bundled into the same offer. Keep it separate so the organic result stays measurable.
