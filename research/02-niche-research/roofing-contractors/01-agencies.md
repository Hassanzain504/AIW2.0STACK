## Agency Profiles

### 1. Hook Agency
**URL:** hookagency.com/roofing-marketing/website-design/
**Positioning:** "Get More Website Traffic And Customers, We Know Roofing Websites."
**Approach:** Content SEO plus Google Ads plus web design. Cites analytics from 100+ contractor client accounts to justify trust-factor and CRO decisions. Publishes cost-per-lead case studies.
**Pricing:** Not publicly listed. [inferred] $1,500-$4,000/month based on scope.

**Pros:**
- Roofing is a primary vertical; not a generalist agency with a roofing landing page
- Data-backed decisions: 100+ client accounts give real benchmark data
- "Built for Google" framing pairs SEO with lead conversion
- Publishes transparent case studies with revenue attribution

**Cons:**
- Price point inaccessible for early-stage contractors
- Sites produced look visually similar across clients; no per-market differentiation
- Copy methodology is traffic-centric; homeowner emotional journey not visible

**What they miss:** Hook optimizes for the click, not the close. No visible voice-of-customer work in client sites. Storm damage urgency, insurance claim navigation, and the homeowner fear of being overcharged are all absent from their template copy.

## Top 3 Winner Patterns

1. **Niche-only positioning wins contractor trust.** Agencies that lead with roofing-specific language in their domain, headline, and case studies close more contractor clients than generalists. Contractors have been burned by generic agencies and are actively skeptical. Niche depth signals you understand their world.

2. **Results framing beats feature framing.** Contractors respond to leads, cost-per-job, and revenue outcomes. Agencies that translate SEO or design into dollars ("Sell More Roofs," revenue claims) outperform those selling traffic or rankings.

3. **Risk reversal reduces the first objection.** Money-back guarantees and performance-based claims lower entry resistance. Any credible accountability mechanism differentiates in a market full of unfulfilled agency promises.

---

## Top 3 Loser Patterns

1. **Ignoring the homeowner conversion layer.** Nearly every agency optimizes for the click, not the close. Websites generate inquiries but the copy, trust signals, and layout do not convert a skeptical homeowner comparing three quotes. The agency wins the contractor client; the homeowner is not won.

2. **Generic stock photography.** Roofing is a visual trade. Real job-site photos, real crew photos, and real before/after imagery outperform stock. Homeowners who have researched on Google Images recognize staged photography. (Pattern observed across Roofing Webmasters, Thrive, and RoofWebs portfolios.)

3. **No emotional specificity in copy.** Storm damage, insurance claims, the fear of being overcharged, a leaking ceiling at 2am: these are the actual decision context when a homeowner calls a roofer. Agencies that produce "quality workmanship, trusted professionals" hero copy miss the emotional entry point entirely.
