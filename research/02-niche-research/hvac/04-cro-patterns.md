# 04 - CRO Patterns: HVAC

Niche: hvac
Module: 2B, Sub-task 4
Sources: raw/agencies-crawl.json, raw/business-homepages.json; cross-referenced with customer voice from Sub-task 2

---

## Recommended Section Order (top to bottom)

1. **Sticky header** — phone number (tap-to-call), "Book Online" button, logo. Always visible.
2. **Hero section** — headline targeting the primary intent (breakdown vs planned maintenance vs replacement), city named, CTA above fold.
3. **Social proof bar** — Google rating + count, NATE certification badge, BBB badge.
4. **Services strip** — AC Repair, Furnace Repair, AC Installation, Furnace Installation, Maintenance Plans, Emergency Service. Clear icons, scannable.
5. **"Available Today" signal** — specific availability message: "We have openings today in [City]. Call before 2pm to guarantee same-day service." Dynamic if possible.
6. **Trust stack** — Years in business, number of systems installed, NATE-certified technician count, warranty details.
7. **Technician profiles** — 2-4 technicians with photo, name, NATE certification, years with the company. Humanizes the service.
8. **How it works** — 3 steps: Book → Diagnostic → Fixed. Removes uncertainty.
9. **Repair vs Replace section** — Plain-language guide with the "5,000 rule" explained honestly. Builds trust before the competitor gets to the quote.
10. **Review section** — 6+ full-text Google reviews with homeowner name, city, and service type.
11. **Financing section** — Monthly payment options with a specific range ("as low as $X/month"). Removes cost barrier for replacement.
12. **Service areas** — Named cities and neighborhoods.
13. **Maintenance plans section** — Plan tiers, what is included, annual savings vs reactive service.
14. **FAQ** — Matches SERP PAA: "$5,000 rule," average AC repair cost, how long systems last, when to replace vs repair.
15. **Final CTA** — Repeat primary CTA with availability signal.
16. **Footer** — License, address, service area, phone, social.

---

## Hero Composition

**Must-haves above the fold:**
- Headline that matches the visitor's intent (breakdown = urgency; planned = trust; replacement = information)
- City or neighborhood named
- Phone number visible; tap-to-call on mobile
- CTA: "Call Now" or "Book Online" (both options, not one)
- Availability signal: "Available Today" or "Technicians in [City] Now"
- Background: photo of an actual technician working on a system; not stock imagery of a family looking comfortable

**Avoid above the fold:**
- System specification tables or SEER ratings (below the fold)
- Multiple competing CTAs
- Brand logos without explanation of what they mean to the homeowner
- Auto-play video backgrounds (slow on mobile; intrusive)

## Trust Stack (priority order)

1. **Google review count and rating** — displayed in hero zone. Format: "4.8 stars — 412 Google Reviews." Linked.
2. **NATE Certification** — NATE (North American Technician Excellence) is the HVAC-specific credential homeowners can look up. Display with explanation: "NATE-certified technicians pass independent exams — your guarantee of qualified work."
3. **Years in business / systems installed** — Specific numbers outperform vague tenure claims.
4. **Licensed, bonded, insured statement** — With actual license number displayed.
5. **Brand certifications** — Carrier Authorized Dealer, Trane Comfort Specialist, Lennox Premier Dealer. These signal training and extended warranty eligibility.
6. **24/7 availability signal** — "We answer 24/7" is a trust signal in HVAC because after-hours availability is rare and valued.
7. **Financing available** — Visible in the hero or trust bar; reduces sticker-shock barrier for replacement visitors.

## Form Patterns

**Best-performing form type for HVAC:** Intent-split form or two-CTA approach.

Because HVAC has two very different visitor intents (emergency = call now; planned = book online), a single form can underperform. Best pattern:

**Primary action:** "Call Now — [Phone Number]" (for emergency/high-urgency)
**Secondary action:** Short form with fields:
- Name
- Phone
- Service type (dropdown: AC Repair / Furnace Repair / New System / Maintenance / Emergency)
- Best time to call

**Form placement:**
- One in hero (short; 3-4 fields max)
- One in the final CTA section
- Avoid forms longer than 5 fields on any HVAC lead page

---

## Sticky and Always-Visible Elements

1. **Sticky header with phone number** — tap-to-call. Non-negotiable on mobile.
2. **Mobile sticky bottom bar** — "Call Now" | "Book Service" — two-button; highest-converting element for mobile HVAC sites.
3. **"Available Today" indicator** — dynamic or static message that communicates real-time availability. If the contractor is available today, say it.
4. **Chat widget** — useful for after-hours qualification. Position bottom-right; do not auto-trigger.

---

## Seasonal CRO Considerations

HVAC has hard seasonal intent peaks: late May/June (AC), and October/November (heating). CRO adjustments by season:

**Summer (AC season):**
- Hero headline references AC and heat
- "Available today" signal is prominent
- Emergency service CTA above fold

**Fall/Winter (heating season):**
- Hero headline references furnace or heat
- "Beat the cold — get your furnace checked" urgency framing
- Maintenance plan CTA elevated

**Shoulder seasons (spring/fall):**
- Maintenance and tune-up angle
- "Get ahead of the season" framing
- Financing for proactive replacement

---

## Mobile-Specific Considerations

- Phone number must be a tappable link above the fold; no exceptions
- Technician photos must load fast; compress to under 100kb
- Financing section must include a monthly payment number (not just "financing available")
- Service area list should be collapsible on mobile; avoid long scrollable lists
- Book Online form must have large touch targets (minimum 44px height per field)
