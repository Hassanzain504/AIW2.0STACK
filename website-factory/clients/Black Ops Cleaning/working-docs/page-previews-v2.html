04 / Money

Who pays for this

In most cases the property insurance does. This is the question we are asked before any other, so here is the straight answer.

Most homeowner policies treat unattended death remediation as a covered loss, because the damage to the property is what is being claimed for. Two things are worth knowing before you call your insurer.

  • Some policies exclude deaths by suicide unless a specific rider was bought. If that applies, we will tell you early rather than late.
  • Ask the adjuster, not the agent. The agent sold the policy. The adjuster decides what it pays, and they can answer in one call.

What we do from there. We speak to the adjuster directly, provide the scope, the photographs and the documentation they need, and bill them. You are not paying up front and claiming it back. If the loss is not covered, we tell you that before we start work, not after.

07 / Questions

Questions people actually ask

Does homeowner insurance cover unattended death cleanup?

In most cases yes, because you are claiming for damage to the property. Some policies exclude deaths by suicide unless a rider was bought. Call your adjuster, not your agent, and ask whether the policy covers biohazard remediation and what the limit is.

Will your vehicle be marked?

No. Our response vehicles carry no company signage and our technicians arrive in plain clothing and change on site. Neighbours see a plain van.

Are you police approved or state certified?

There is no such approval or certification to hold in Maryland, and any company claiming one is worth a second look. What matters is GBAC training, OSHA bloodborne pathogen compliance, a licensed disposal route, and being licensed and insured in Maryland. We are all four.

02 / Local hook

Why cleaning in Rockville is not generic office cleaning

Rockville sits at the centre of the I-270 Biotech Corridor, and that changes what a cleaning contract here actually has to do.

Over 120 of Montgomery County's roughly 350 life sciences firms have a Rockville address. More than 10,000 people work in biohealth along the corridor, alongside an estimated 49,000 in federal agencies, with NIH and the FDA on the doorstep.

A large share of the buildings here are not open plan offices. They are labs, GMP manufacturing space, medical suites and federal adjacent facilities, and those come with rules an ordinary janitorial contract does not cover.

  • Controlled areas you do not enter without protocol. Cleaning around a lab is not the same job as cleaning a lab.
  • Inspection schedules that decide when you can work. Audit calendars, not your rota.
  • Documented infection control, because a failed inspection costs a practice far more than a cleaning contract.
  • Background checked crews for federal adjacent buildings.

Rockville's older stock has the opposite problem. Town Center and the Pike corridor carry buildings from several decades of construction, and a 1980s office fitout does not clean like a 2020s lab building. Both are within ten minutes of us.
