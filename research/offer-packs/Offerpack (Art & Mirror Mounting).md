1. "He knew exactly how to balance the gallery wall without making it look crammed."
2. "She hung twelve pieces in two hours and not a single hole was wrong."
3. "That mirror is huge and heavy. I wouldn't trust anyone else to put it up."
4. "He came with all the right anchors for our plaster walls. No damage at all."
5. "She helped us figure out the spacing before she touched a nail."
6. "I tried to do it myself and made three holes. He fixed all of them and got it right."
7. "Recommended the right height for the sofa. Makes the whole room look bigger."
8. "They work with our designer and always show up prepared."

---

## Top 3 trust elements to name in every DM

1. **Before-and-after gallery wall photos** (this service is entirely visual. homeowners need to see finished work to feel confident handing over expensive art and freshly painted walls)
2. **Wall type expertise (plaster, drywall, brick, tile)** (the biggest homeowner fear is damage. naming the wall types you handle and the anchors you use turns that fear into confidence)
3. **Designer referral mentions** (interior designers are a repeat-order pipeline. showing that designers trust you signals professionalism to both designers and their clients)

## The one outreach rule

Send the short DM first on Instagram or LinkedIn. If no reply in 4 days, send the medium version to their email. If no reply in another 5 days, send the long version with the Starbucks offer. Three touches per prospect, then move on.

Do not pitch. Ask for 15 minutes. The goal of the DM is not to sell a website. The goal is to get a screen-share call where you show them the free design.
