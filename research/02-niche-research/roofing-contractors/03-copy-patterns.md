## Top 10 Hero Headlines

These are patterns observed across agency sites and top-ranking contractor pages, cross-referenced with what homeowner reviews tell us actually matters.

1. "Licensed Roofers Who Handle the Insurance So You Don't Have To"
   (addresses insurance fear directly; high frequency theme in reviews)

2. "Roof Replacement in [City] — Done Right, Backed by a 10-Year Workmanship Warranty"
   (combines local specificity with warranty risk-reversal)

3. "Emergency Roof Repair Available Today — [City]'s Trusted Roofers Since [Year]"
   (urgency + trust + local; targets emergency repair search intent)

4. "Free Roof Inspection. Honest Report. No Pressure."
   (directly addresses the fear of being misled; "honest" appears 40+ times in reviews)

5. "Your Roof Fixed Once. No Callbacks, No Leaks, No Surprises."
   (specificity about the failure mode homeowners fear most)

6. "Storm Damage? We Work Directly With Your Insurance Adjuster."
   (insurance claim assistance framed as a service, not a side note)

7. "Roof Replacement Starting at $X,XXX — See Exact Pricing Before We Start"
   (pricing transparency as trust signal; addresses "being overcharged" fear)

8. "[City] Roofers With 500+ Five-Star Reviews — Read Them Here"
   (social proof volume in the headline itself)

9. "We Show Up, We Clean Up, We Follow Up — Guaranteed"
   (addresses the three biggest complaint categories in negative reviews)

10. "From Free Inspection to Final Cleanup — One Crew, One Contact, No Subcontractors"
    (addresses crew continuity and accountability fear)

1. "Get Your Free Roof Inspection" — primary CTA; low friction entry point
2. "Call [Phone Number] Now — We Answer 24/7" — for emergency intent
3. "See Our Work — Photo Gallery" — visual proof before committing
4. "Check Our Reviews on Google" — directs to third-party trust
5. "Get a Quote in 24 Hours" — specific timeline reduces uncertainty
6. "Download Our Free Homeowner's Roof Guide" — lead magnet for research-phase visitors
7. "Schedule Your Free Inspection This Week" — urgency without pressure
8. "See What [City] Homeowners Are Saying" — social proof CTA

## Top 10 Value Props (what homeowners actually want, from review data)

1. **Shows up when promised.** "On time" appears 73 times in reviews; its absence is the most common negative trigger.

2. **Handles insurance claims.** 98 insurance mentions; framing the contractor as the homeowner's insurance ally is a major differentiator.

3. **Explains the problem clearly.** Reviews repeatedly praise contractors who "explained what they saw" vs those who just hand over a quote.

4. **Leaves the site clean.** "Clean" / "kept it clean" appears 104 times. Debris removal is an explicit expectation.

5. **Uses a named, consistent crew.** "Crew" appears 152 times; homeowners evaluate who is on their property.

6. **Communicates throughout the job.** "Communication" 69 times; project manager check-ins are specifically praised.

7. **Gives a fair, itemized quote.** Review language shows the quote interaction is a trust test; the contractor who explains line items wins.

8. **Finishes in the timeframe promised.** "Replacement done in one day" is used as a positive signal across multiple reviews.

9. **Backs the work with a warranty.** Post-job failure is the nightmare scenario; warranty language converts skeptical homeowners.

10. **Is easy to reach after the job.** Follow-through is cited in the highest-impact reviews and referrals.

## Copy Angles to Avoid

- "Quality workmanship you can trust" — generic; every competitor says this
- "Family-owned and operated" — used so frequently it has lost meaning
- "Serving [City] for X years" — fine as a supporting detail, not a hero claim
- "Free estimates" — table stakes; not a differentiator
- Any claim about being "#1" without a citation source
