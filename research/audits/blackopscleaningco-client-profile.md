## 2. How long has this business been running

**Straight answer: not fully confirmed, but everything found points to it being recent, likely
2024.**

Evidence:
- A Facebook post exists titled "Introducing Black Ops Cleaning Co. We're proud to announce the
  soft launch of..." This is a soft-launch announcement post, not a years-old established
  presence. The exact date could not be pulled from search results alone; open that post
  directly on Facebook to get it.
- The homepage image folder path recovered during the technical crawl was
  `/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/...`, meaning the current website's images were first uploaded
  in October 2024. That is a reasonable proxy for when the current site went live.
- The site's own copy says "30+ years of combined experience", which is a team/founder
  experience claim, not a company age claim. The founder's own bio (Army service, then
  JPMorgan Chase, then this company) does not read as a company with a long operating history.
- No historical review trail exists anywhere. Angi lists them as a new business with no
  reviews. Facebook shows zero reviews. If the company had been operating for several years,
  some review trail would normally exist even from a slow starter.

**Action:** ask the client directly, in week 1, what the actual founding date and first-client
date were. This matters for two reasons. It sets what "years in business" claim, if any, can
honestly go on the site (currently the site makes no explicit age claim, which is itself
consistent with being young). And it affects the review target: a business open under two years
starting from zero reviews is a completely normal, explainable story to tell customers when
asking for a review. A business open ten years with zero reviews is a much harder story.

cleaning companies with the same last name, it is worth a direct, low-key question to the
client: is there any family or prior working relationship with AMR US. This does not change the
plan either way, but it is the kind of thing better asked upfront than discovered later.

## 6. What to actually do with this list

1. **Open each Facebook and Instagram link yourself and record**: follower count, post
   frequency (active in the last 30 days or dead), review count and rating if shown, and how
   recent their photos are. This document gives you the links, not the numbers, because several
   of them need a logged-in view or change too often to keep static in a file.
2. **The Rockville set is crowded** (9 named competitors). This confirms the earlier
   recommendation to lead with the specialty lane, where the field is thinner: Bio-One of
   Gaithersburg is the only close-proximity direct specialty competitor, and the regional
   players (Tri State, SI Restoration, Crime Scene Inc) compete on content depth rather than
   proximity.
3. **Green Apple Cleaning and AMR US show up in nearly every town.** These two are the client's
   real broad-market competitors across the whole ring, not just one town. Worth a closer
   individual look: pricing if published, review count, and what their town pages actually say,
   since several of them (Green Apple, AMR US, Crime Scene Clean Up) already run the exact
   per-town page structure recommended in the 14-page plan.
4. **Confirm the GBP question directly**, per section 3, before finalizing any pricing or
   timeline. It is the single fact that most changes the shape of the first month of work.
