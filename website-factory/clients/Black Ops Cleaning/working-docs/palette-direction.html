Palette direction
Pre Stage 7

Two palettes,
and where each colour goes

One palette has to carry all 57 pages. The same ten colours sit behind a janitorial contract page and behind an unattended death page. That single fact decides almost everything below, and it rules out the two palettes most people would reach for first.

FIRST

This is direction, not the final palette

The factory derives the real palette in Stage 7. The design-synthesis skill reads the client's logo, pulls its dominant colours, and builds the tokens from them. Black Ops has not supplied a logo yet, so nothing here is final. When the logo arrives, Stage 7 overwrites this.

What this page is for: deciding the direction now, so the logo conversation starts from somewhere rather than from nothing. Both options below are built to the exact ten token slots in references/brand-dna.shape.js, so either one drops straight into brand-dna.json.

Why this page is grey

The other working documents use a brick red accent. This one does not. A red eyebrow next to a navy swatch tilts the comparison before you have looked at anything. The chrome here is deliberately neutral so both palettes are judged on their own.

RULED OUT

Two obvious choices that do not work

Worth stating plainly, because both will come up again later and the reasoning should be on record.

Red, however tempting on an emergency service

Red is the reflex for 24 hour response, and on a plumbing site it would be right. Here it is not. On a biohazard or trauma page, red reads as blood.

The copy decks are deliberate about never sensationalising the event. The crime scene page carries an explicit instruction: no urgency language, no sales pressure, no adjectives about what happened. A red palette undoes all of that in the first second, before a word is read.

Tactical black and neon green, because of the name

The name invites a special forces look. The audience forbids it.

The hoarding page is read by a son or a daughter who is frightened for a parent, and that page is written so the person living there could read it too without feeling judged. A tactical palette makes that page feel like an intervention and makes the trauma page feel like a game. The veteran angle is a real asset, but it is carried by the words and by the registrations, not by the colour scheme.

And one that simply failed the maths

Slate with an evergreen accent was tested and dropped. Green signals clean and safe, which fits, but the accent scored 2.2 against the primary used as text on it, well under the 4.5 the schema requires. Every cleaning company in the teardown uses green anyway, so it would have made Black Ops look like its competitors.

Why it holds

Navy is the colour of service, and it is quiet. Brass is the language of insignia rather than of hazard, so it reads as earned rather than as alarming. On the right hand mock the palette gets out of the way, which is exactly what that page needs. Nothing here says emergency, and nothing here says military operation.

Why it holds, and where it is riskier

Amber comes from hazard signage, which is honest for biohazard work and warm rather than alarming. The risk is that graphite plus amber sits closer to the construction and restoration category, so it differentiates less from a disaster restoration firm than navy does. On the specialty mock it is slightly warmer than option A, which some will prefer and some will find too present.

OPEN

What cannot be decided yet

The logo, which outranks everything on this page

Stage 7 builds the palette from the logo's dominant colours. If Black Ops already has a logo in a particular colour, that colour wins over both options here. Get the logo file before spending any more time on this.

The fonts, which are blocked further upstream

The four typography slots are still empty, and they should stay empty for now. design-synthesis picks fonts from a locked roster in the niche playbook, and Module 2D writes that roster. It has not run. Choosing a font today means choosing it again later.

What you can do now

Pick a direction, A or C, and take it into the logo conversation with the client. A logo designed against a known palette is a much shorter conversation than a logo designed in a vacuum and then fought with.

Method. Token names and the ten required slots from website-factory/references/brand-dna.shape.js. The contrast rules, that primary must pass white text and that the accent must pass the primary used as text on it, from the design-synthesis skill's palette synthesis pass. Every ratio above was computed against the WCAG relative luminance formula, not estimated. The reader constraints come from the tone rules written into copy deck batches one and two, specifically the crime scene page's no urgency language rule and the hoarding page's no judgement rule.

This is pre Stage 7 direction. It is not a locked palette, it has not been near a logo, and the factory will regenerate it properly once one exists.
