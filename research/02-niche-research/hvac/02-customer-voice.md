## Decision Moments

1. **System failure.** The AC stops on a 95-degree day. The furnace dies on the coldest night of the year. The decision is urgent, emotional, and not price-rational. Whoever answers the phone first and can come today wins.

2. **The seasonal tune-up.** A homeowner proactively schedules maintenance before summer or winter. This is a lower-urgency moment but high lifetime-value entry point. Homeowners in this mode are choosing a contractor relationship, not just a job.

3. **The "do I repair or replace?" crossroads.** System is 10-15 years old. Technician diagnoses a $1,500 repair. Homeowner wonders if they are being sold a repair when they should replace, or a replacement when a repair would do. The contractor who explains the decision framework — honestly, with options — converts and earns loyalty.

4. **The post-failure replacement.** System is dead. Homeowner needs a new system. $8,000-$15,000 decision. Multiple quotes. The contractor whose site gives the best information about what to expect, what questions to ask, and what the process looks like has an advantage before the technician arrives.

5. **The maintenance agreement renewal.** Existing customer decides whether to continue a service plan. Contractor who has communicated proactively throughout the year retains; those who only call when it is time to pay lose.

---

## Top 5 Fears

1. **Being upsold an unnecessary replacement.** The "$5,000 rule for HVAC" appears in SERP PAA questions three times. Homeowners know this heuristic (repair cost x 5 < replacement cost = repair; otherwise replace). They fear a contractor will recommend replacement when repair is appropriate. Addressable with honest diagnostic framing and published pricing transparency.

2. **System failing after the repair.** The AC was "fixed" and broke again two weeks later. Wasted money, wasted time, still hot. Addressable via workmanship guarantees and follow-up communication after service.

3. **Not understanding what they are buying.** HVAC systems are opaque to most homeowners. SEER ratings, tonnage, heat pumps vs furnaces: the homeowner who does not understand their options is a homeowner who does not trust the contractor. Addressable via plain-language explainer content.

4. **Overpriced service calls.** "Ridiculously overpriced" is the full text of the 2-star review in the corpus. No specifics, but the emotion is clear. Homeowners have no benchmark for HVAC pricing. Transparent pricing pages, flat-rate service call fees, and published financing options reduce this fear.

5. **Not being available when it matters.** Broken AC on a Sunday. Furnace out on a holiday. The fear of calling and getting no answer. Addressable via after-hours availability, AI call answering, and specific language: "We answer 24/7 — no voicemail."
