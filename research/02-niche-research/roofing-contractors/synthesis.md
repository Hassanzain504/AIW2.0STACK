NICHE SYNTHESIS, roofing-contractors

End customer in one sentence: A homeowner facing a failing or storm-damaged roof who fears being overcharged, misled, or left with recurring leaks, and who defaults to Google reviews and word-of-mouth to choose a contractor.

The student's client in one sentence: A residential roofing contractor running 2-5 crews, doing $800K-$2.5M in annual revenue, currently buying shared leads from Angi or Thumbtack and looking for an owned channel with better margins.

Top 5 insights:

1. Insurance navigation is a primary product, not a side feature. 98 of 663 reviews explicitly mention insurance. In storm-damage markets (Dallas, Phoenix, Chicago), contractors who handle the claim process are chosen over those who don't, even at higher quoted prices. The website must present this as a service with its own section and CTA.

2. The homeowner's trust crisis starts at the quote visit. Reviews use "honest" and "explained" more than any other quality descriptor. The biggest single converter on a roofing site is not the design; it is evidence that the contractor will tell the truth during the inspection.

3. Aggregators own "near me" searches; contractors must win locally. BBB, Angi, Thumbtack, and Yelp dominate bare "near me" SERPs. A contractor website wins by targeting city + service and neighborhood queries, owning GBP, and publishing local cost content that aggregators cannot match.

4. The gap in every agency's work is the homeowner conversion layer. Every agency from Hook to Thrive optimizes for traffic and lead volume. None produce copy or design specifically architected around the homeowner's fear hierarchy (overcharged, poor workmanship, poor communication, insurance complexity). This is the student's differentiation.

5. Real photos convert better than stock imagery in this niche. Roofing is a credibility-visual trade. Homeowners evaluating crews they will allow on their homes respond to actual job-site photos. Stock photography is a visible gap in every agency portfolio reviewed.

Top 3 risks if we pick this niche:

- Storm chaser agencies and national aggregators dominate the market; it is hard to stand out without real photo assets from the client.
- Client quality varies widely; a roofing contractor with no reviews or a bad reputation cannot be rescued by a good website.
- The niche is seasonal in northern markets; revenue concentration in spring-fall means clients may churn or pause in winter.

Top 3 reasons to pick this niche:

- Average ticket is $9,500-$12,000 for a replacement. One extra job per month from the website covers the build cost in week one, making ROI easy to demonstrate.
- Insurance work de-risks the homeowner's purchase; contractors who offer insurance claim navigation have a defensible differentiator that a well-built website can amplify.
- There are 100+ marketing agencies serving this niche but almost none producing homeowner-centric copy and CRO. The gap is real and exploitable.

Money math headline: "A roofing contractor closing two extra replacement jobs per month from their own website covers the site cost in the first week and every job after that is pure margin."
