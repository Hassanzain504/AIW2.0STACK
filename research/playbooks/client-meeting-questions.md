# Client Meeting: Opening Questions

The order matters more than the questions. Let them talk first. The audit proves you did the
homework, but if you open by presenting, you are guessing at what they care about. Ask first,
present second, and the presentation lands on what they already told you matters.

Rough shape of the meeting:

| Phase | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Open | 2 min | Set the frame |
| 2a. Why you are here | 5 min | Why they said yes |
| 2b. Their business | 10 min | They talk, you listen |
| 3. Their goals | 5 min | What they actually want |
| 4. The numbers | 5 min | Job values and close rate |
| 5. The gating questions | 5 min | Decisions only they can make |
| 6. Show the audit | 15 min | Now it lands |
| 7. What you need from them | 5 min | Reviews, photos, access |
| 8. Close | 3 min | Next step |

---

## Phase 1. Open

Do not open with your credentials. Open with why you are there and what they get out of the
next hour, whether or not they buy.

> "Thanks for the time. Before I show you anything, I want to understand the business properly,
> because half of what I found only matters depending on what you actually want more of.
>
> I have gone through your website line by line and looked at nine competitors across Montgomery
> County. Whatever you decide at the end, you will leave this call knowing exactly where you
> stand against them. That part is yours either way."

That last sentence does a lot of work. It removes the pressure and it makes them listen.

---

## Phase 2a. Why you are in the room

Ask this before anything else. It is the highest value question in the meeting, and almost
nobody asks it.

1. **"Before I get into anything, you must get a lot of these emails. What made you say yes to
   this one?"**

   Their answer tells you what to emphasise for the next forty minutes. Listen carefully and
   let them finish.

   | What they say | What it means | What you do |
   |---|---|---|
   | "You mentioned [specific thing]" | Your positioning landed. That exact thing is their sore point. | Lead the audit with it |
   | "I have been meaning to sort the website out" | They already know. Low education needed. | Skip the convincing, go to the plan |
   | "My competitor's site looks better than mine" | Competitive pain, which is the strongest kind | Open with the scoreboard |
   | "I am not getting enough calls" | Lead volume is the pain | Lead with Google Maps |
   | "Honestly I just had a free slot" | Low intent | Qualify harder before you invest the hour |
   | Vague or deflecting | They may not be the decision maker, or they are just polite | Ask question 4 |

2. **"How many of these do you get in a week?"**
   Small question, useful answer. If it is ten a week and they took yours, something in it
   worked and you want to know what. It also tells you how much noise you are competing with
   next time.

3. **"Have you taken any of these calls before? How did they go?"**
   This surfaces objections before you walk into them. If they were burned by an agency, you
   need to know now rather than at the price. It may also explain the 141 referring domains.

4. **"Is there something going on right now that made this the right time, or has it been on
   the list for a while?"**
   Urgency check. A lost bid, a slow quarter, a competitor winning work they wanted, or a
   government contract they are preparing for. Any of those changes how you frame everything
   that follows.

5. **"And just so I pitch this at the right level, is this decision yours or is there anyone
   else involved?"**
   Ask it plainly, near the end of this phase. Better to know now than after you have presented
   for forty minutes.

---

## Phase 2b. Their business

Let them run. Take notes. Do not correct anything yet.

6. **"Walk me through the business. How did it start?"**
   You already know the answer: Army, then JPMorgan Chase, then ten years alongside his father
   in the family cleaning business. Ask anyway. People tell you what they are proud of, and
   that is what goes on the About page.

7. **"How long have you been running Black Ops specifically?"**
   Evidence points to around October 2024. Confirm it. This decides whether "zero reviews" is a
   normal new-business story or a problem to explain.

8. **"Who is on the team right now?"**
   Tells you capacity. A two-person operation cannot take on a hospital contract next month, and
   your plan should not pretend otherwise.

9. **"What does a typical week look like? What kind of jobs are you actually running?"**
   Listen for the split between janitorial and specialty. It may not match the website at all.

10. **"Which towns have you actually worked in so far?"**
   **This is the single most important operational question in the meeting.** It caps the town
   pages. If the answer is five towns, you build five, not seventeen.

11. **"Where do your jobs come from today?"**
   Referral, word of mouth, Angi, a bid. If they say "mostly word of mouth", that is your
   opening later: word of mouth is a ceiling, not a strategy.

12. **"Have you worked with anyone on marketing before?"**
   Tells you whether they are burned, and it may explain the 141 referring domains.

---

## Phase 3. Their goals

13. **"Twelve months from now, what has to be true for you to say this year went well?"**
   Open ended on purpose. Let them define success before you define it for them.

14. **"Which kind of work do you want more of? Janitorial contracts, specialty and biohazard, or
   government?"**
   The whole plan hinges on this. Do not assume. The website says one thing, the certifications
   say another, and the SAM.gov registration says a third.

15. **"Is there work you are turning down, or work you wish you were getting called for?"**
    Often the most revealing question in the whole meeting.

16. **"What is the biggest constraint right now? Not enough leads, not enough crew, or cash?"**
    If the answer is crew or cash, more leads is not the problem and you should know that before
    you sell.

---

## Phase 4. The numbers

Say why you are asking, then ask. People give numbers more freely when they know what it is for.

> "I want to give you a straight answer on whether this is worth doing, so I need three numbers.
> Rough is fine."

17. **"What is an average janitorial contract worth to you per month?"**
18. **"What is an average biohazard or specialty job worth?"**
19. **"Out of the quotes you send, roughly what share close?"**

With those three you can do the break-even out loud, using their numbers rather than industry
averages. That single move is worth more than any slide.

20. **"How long is the sales cycle on a commercial contract? Walkthrough to signature."**
    Sets expectations for month three, before they set them for you.

---

## Phase 5. The gating questions

Five decisions only they can make. Each one changes the work.

21. **"Do you have a Google Business Profile set up?"**
    Ask it plainly. Everything you found says no. If the answer is "I think so", ask them to
    search the business name in Google Maps while you are both on the call.

22. **"Will you answer the phone at 3am?"**
    Frame it honestly: *"Every biohazard competitor in this market advertises 24/7. If you will
    not, I am not going to build emergency pages, because they will rank and then convert at
    nothing. There is no wrong answer, I just need to know."*

23. **"Are you commercial and specialty, or do you also want residential?"**
    Their metadata currently sells residential. Their business does not. Get the decision on
    record.

24. **"Where are you with the veteran-owned certification, and is SAM.gov still active?"**
    You know they are registered, UEI PMPPFTB7CCX4. Ask about the certification stage and
    whether registration has been renewed. This is the highest ceiling item in their business.

25. **"Any connection to AMR US Commercial Cleaning? I noticed the same surname."**
    Ask casually, near the end of this phase. Probably a coincidence. Better asked now than
    discovered later.

---

## Phase 6. Show the audit

Now present. Because of phases 2 to 5, you can tie every finding back to something they said.

Order that works:
1. The scoreboard. Where they stand against three measured competitors.
2. The AI visibility number. Zero across all four engines. Nobody else is talking about this.
3. The citations finding. They have links, they do not have pages, and most agencies would sell
   them the wrong thing.
4. Google Maps. The single biggest gap.
5. The website faults, quickly. Lead with the federal agency logos, because it is a real risk
   they did not know about and it proves you actually looked.

Ask once, in the middle:

26. **"Does any of this surprise you?"**
    Their answer tells you whether they already knew and could not act, or genuinely did not
    know. Those are two different conversations.

---

## Phase 7. What you need from them

Do this before the price, not after. It makes the price feel like part of a partnership rather
than a transaction.

27. **"Reviews only work if they come from you. Will you personally call twenty past clients?"**
    Do not soften this. If the answer is no, the map plan caps out and you both need to know
    now.

28. **"Can your crew photograph every job? Five photos a week is what unlocks each new town
    page."**

29. **"Who has the logins? Google, hosting, domain, the website."**
    If nobody knows, that is your first task and it can take a week.

30. **"How fast can you turn around drafts?"**
    Sets the delivery expectation honestly.

---

## Phase 8. Close

31. **"What is your reaction so far?"**
    Silence after this. Do not fill it.

32. **"What would need to be true for you to want to start?"**
    Surfaces the real objection instead of the polite one.

33. **"If you want to go ahead, the first thing I need is access. Can we get that sorted this
    week?"**
    Ask for a specific, small next step, not a decision.

---

## Things to avoid

- **Do not open with the price.** It is at the end of the proposal deliberately.
- **Do not promise rankings, leads or revenue.** Say it out loud once: *"I will not promise you
  a ranking position or a lead count. Anyone who does is guessing with your money."*
- **Do not read the audit aloud.** They can read. Talk about what it means.
- **Do not correct them mid-answer.** If they say something the audit contradicts, note it and
  come back to it in phase 6.
- **Do not fill silences.** After a price or a hard question, wait.

## Three things to say out loud, because they are not in the documents any more

1. **The guarantee.** *"At day 30, if the homepage is not under two seconds on mobile, the Google
   profile is not complete, and the pages are not live, you do not pay the second half."*
2. **What is not included.** Paid ads, photography, the government contracting page, and any
   third-party costs like call tracking.
3. **No outcome promises.** Rankings and leads depend on Google's timing and on the reviews and
   photographs only they can supply.

Put all three in the contract as well.
