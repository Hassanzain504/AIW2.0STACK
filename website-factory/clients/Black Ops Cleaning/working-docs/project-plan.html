02

The decision that unblocks everything

Two honest routes. They differ in what the second cleaning client costs you, not in whether Black Ops gets delivered.

Route AHand build

Build Black Ops by hand now. Formalise the factory afterwards from what you learned.

  • Starts today. No setup, no credentials, no gates.
  • 57 pages by hand. Realistically you ship the 14 and the rolling 43 slip.
  • Nothing is reusable. The next cleaning client costs the same again.
  • The stack stays at zero and gets harder to start the busier you get.
Route BRecommended

Run the stack now with the niche already decided. Black Ops is the proof that commercial and specialty cleaning is the niche. Build the template, then let the factory produce the pages.

  • Modules 1 to 2C compress to about a day. You are not discovering a niche, you are recording a decision you already made and can evidence.
  • Module 2D is the real work, and it is the thing that pays off forever.
  • It runs in parallel with proposal engagement one, which is technical fixes, Google profile and reviews. None of that needs the factory.
  • Client two onwards is a data change, not a rebuild.

Take Route B, and start it before the client says yes

The 30 day clock starts at access handover, not today. Every day between now and that handover is free time to get the factory to the point where it can build. If the client signs in a week, you want the commercial cleaning template already scaffolded when they do.

If they do not sign, you still own a cleaning niche template and the next nine prospects in Montgomery County are the same pitch. Nothing is wasted either way.

Two rules that decide whether any of this works

Identical details everywhere, character for character. Inconsistent name, address or phone actively confuses Google and can do more harm than filing nothing. This is why the reference sheet comes first and why the missing website address gets fixed before a single listing is created.

Tier two beats twenty general directories combined. The veteran and industry listings are relevant, hard for competitors to copy, and point at the exact buyers the client wants. If time runs short, cut tier three, never tier two.
