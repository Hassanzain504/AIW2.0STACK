## Stage of business

**Stage: No portfolio**

Hassan has 4 years of freelance web dev experience but no roofing-specific case studies and no existing client in this niche. The correct offer shape is:

"I'll design your homepage upfront, for free. If you love it, you pay. If not, you walk. No invoice, no awkward follow-up."

This removes all risk from the prospect's side. The first 1-2 clients are bought with time, not discounted on price. Once one client is live and producing results, move to Stage 2.

## Offer mechanics

**Risk reversal:** Free homepage design upfront. Paid on approval only. First client pays nothing until they see the design and say yes.

**Price anchor (3-tier, they buy the middle):**

| Tier | What they get | Price |
|---|---|---|
| Starter | Homepage only, no SEO, 2 revision rounds | $800 |
| Growth (recommend this) | Full 5-page site, basic local SEO, before/after damage gallery, insurance walkthrough section, mobile-optimized | $1,800 |
| Pro | Full site + GBP optimization + 3-month SEO content plan + automation setup (speed-to-lead form + auto-reply) | $3,500 |

**Payment terms (Stage 1):** Free design upfront. On approval: 50% deposit, 50% on launch. After first case study: 30% deposit, 70% on launch.

**The Starbucks guarantee:** "If I waste your time, I'll send you a $25 Starbucks gift card. At least let me show you where I see the missed opportunities."

## Top 3 trust elements to name in every DM

From roofing review analysis:

1. **Before/after photo gallery with damage-to-finished pairs** (homeowners need to see proof of the transformation -- shingle damage to clean new roof -- not a logo or a stock photo of a house)
2. **Insurance claim process walkthrough** (the #1 anxiety for storm-damage customers is not understanding how the claim works -- no competitor explains this on the homepage; this is the student's primary differentiator)
3. **Named crew with license number and insurance visible** (roofers work on the most expensive part of the house; homeowners need to know who is coming before they say yes)

### Prospect 5: Any roofing contractor with 20 to 60 Google reviews, active on Facebook, but a weak or missing website
**How to find them:** Search "roofing contractor [target US city]" on Google Maps. Sort by rating. Look for contractors with 3.9 to 4.5 stars and 20 to 60 reviews. Check their website link. If it's missing, redirects to Facebook, or hasn't been updated in 3+ years, they are the ideal first prospect.
**Personalization angle:** Match what their reviews say. If reviews mention "Jason explained the whole process" -- their site probably doesn't introduce Jason. If reviews say "cleaned up better than they found it" -- their site probably has no crew section. Mirror the review language back at them.
**DM version:** Medium, with specific review quote inserted.

## The one outreach rule

Send the short DM first on Instagram or LinkedIn. If no reply in 4 days, send the medium version to their email. If no reply in another 5 days, send the long version with the Starbucks offer. Three touches per prospect, then move on.

Do not pitch. Ask for 15 minutes. The goal of the DM is not to sell a website. The goal is to get a screen-share call where you show them the free design.
