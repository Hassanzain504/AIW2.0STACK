The review system, and what it actually is

Not software. A routine built into how you close a job, so reviews arrive steadily instead of never.

Reviews do two separate jobs. First, they decide whether Google shows you in the map at all. Review count and how regularly new ones arrive are among the strongest factors there. Second, they decide whether anyone calls. Three businesses appear in the map block and a facility manager compares three star ratings in about four seconds. With zero reviews you lose that comparison every time, even when you rank.

What gets built:

  • Your real target, worked out from the review counts of the businesses currently in the map pack for each keyword and town. Not a round number, the actual number you need to beat.
  • A direct review link, shortened, plus a QR code for the van and for invoices.
  • The ask, written out: what the technician says on site at completion, the text message that follows within the hour, and the single reminder at day three.
  • Response templates for five, three and one star reviews, and the first month of replies handled for you.
  • Facebook, BBB and Yelp opened as review channels alongside Google.

The part only you can do

Business to business reviews come from a phone call, not an automation. The first twenty need to come from you personally calling clients you have already served. I build the system, write every script and template, and handle every reply. The calls are yours, and they are what decides how fast this moves.

What waiting costs

Doing nothing is also a decision, and it has a price.

Every one of these is running against you today

  • You are not on Google Maps. Every local search shows a competitor's listing and not yours. That happens every day, at no cost to them.
  • You rank for nothing. Zero search terms. Your competitors hold 877, 1,400 and 3,900.
  • No AI engine cites you. Zero across ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Google AI Mode and Gemini. One competitor has 152 pages cited.
  • Your review count stays at zero, and reviews compound, so the gap widens every month rather than holding still.
  • Federal agency logos are still on your homepage while you bid for federal work.

On the conservative model in the audit, that is roughly one job a month going elsewhere. Waiting a quarter to decide is not neutral. It is the price of this proposal, again, in work that went to somebody else.
