The 24 hour question, decided

You asked me to make this call. Here it is, with the reasoning, and the one thing that has to be true for it to ship.

The call: answer the phone 24 hours, state the on-site window separately

Do not write "24/7 crews standing by". Write "we answer 24 hours" and then give the on-site response window as its own honest number.

Those are two different promises and every competitor blurs them. Blurring them is how a small operator ends up with a one star review that says nobody came.

Why this way

Not answering is not an option. The audit found the site sells emergency cleaning and sewage decontamination while listing hours of Monday to Friday, 9 to 5. Every biohazard competitor in this market advertises around the clock availability. These calls do not arrive on Tuesday at 11am. They arrive at 2am, and they go to whoever picks up. A 9 to 5 listing on an emergency service is not caution, it is a leak.

But over-promising is worse than under-promising. A one man veteran owned operation cannot credibly claim crews standing by. The first time somebody calls at 3am and gets voicemail, that is a review, and reviews are the thing this whole engagement is trying to build.

The honest version is also the stronger one. "We answer 24 hours, and we are usually on site within two hours" is a specific, checkable claim. "24/7 emergency response" is a phrase every competitor uses and nobody reads. Specific beats loud.

The one thing that has to be true

Somebody genuinely answers. That does not have to be Amilcar at 3am. An answering service that takes the address and pages him satisfies the promise, costs very little, and is what most operators this size actually run.

If the answer is a hard no, tell me and I will rewrite these four pages: every availability claim comes out, the emergency response service page does not get built, and the specialty pages lead on the written-price-first promise instead of on speed. That is a weaker position against Bio-One and PuroClean, but it is honest, and I would rather ship that than a promise the crew cannot keep.

Block 02, the problem

The mistake that costs people twice

Sewage is classified as Category 3 water, which the restoration industry calls black water. It carries E. coli, salmonella, hepatitis A, norovirus, rotavirus and cryptosporidium.

Here is what matters, and it is the reason people end up paying for this job twice. Those pathogens soak into drywall, carpet, insulation and wood within minutes, and they survive on and inside those materials long after the water has gone. Drying a room does not remove them. It only removes the evidence that they are there.

The IICRC S500 standard, which is the industry standard for water damage restoration, does not permit porous materials to be restored after Category 3 contamination. They come out and go to regulated disposal. That is not our preference, it is the standard.

If you have a quote in front of you that talks about drying equipment and dehumidifiers without mentioning removing anything, ask the company which category of water they think this is. The answer will tell you a great deal.

This block is the whole page. It is technically true, it is checkable, and it directly disqualifies the cheaper quote the reader is holding. No competitor page checked in this market explains it.

Block 06, insurance

Who pays for this, and why this one is different

Most standard homeowner policies do not cover sewer and drain backup. This is the opposite of what people expect, and it is the single most important thing on this page.

Backup cover is usually sold as a separate endorsement or rider. If it was never added to the policy, the cleanup is out of pocket. That is a hard thing to be told and we would rather tell you before we start work than after.

Call your adjuster and ask one question: do I have sewer or water backup coverage on this policy, and what is the limit. The limits on these endorsements are often lower than people assume, so ask for the number as well as the yes or no.

Two more things worth knowing:

  • If the backup came from the municipal main rather than from inside your property, there may be a claim against the utility or the county. Worth asking about, and worth photographing everything before it is touched.
  • Insurers expect IICRC S500 method on Category 3 losses. A claim handled to that standard, with documentation, is far less likely to be argued about than one where a company dried the room and hoped.

Where cover exists, we deal with the adjuster and bill them directly.
