# Lessons: 05-copy-deck agent (landscaping niche)

These rules take precedence over the agent spec. Apply on every landscaping copy run.

---

## Voice register

Family-warm, not corporate. Short sentences. Plain words. No buzzwords.

Blocked words (add to the universal blocklist for this niche): seamless, robust, leverage, stunning, cutting-edge, game-changer, transformative, elevate, innovative, passionate, dedicated, excellence, solutions.

No em-dashes anywhere. Use a plain comma, a period, or rewrite the sentence.

---

## Customer language to echo verbatim

Pull these phrases from the review corpus and mirror them in copy. Do not paraphrase.

1. "bringing our vision to life"
2. "the transformation is amazing"
3. "beautiful" (highest aspiration signal in 484 reviews, use it deliberately)
4. "responsive throughout the entire process"
5. "honest about pricing and gave us a lot of options"
6. "professional and had great communication"
7. "he gave us great ideas for long-term projects"
8. "the project manager explained the materials and the labor"
9. "on time every day"
10. "cleaned up after themselves"

---

## Section-by-section rules

Hero: The homeowner's yard transformation is achievable here. This contractor is local, real, and can be trusted. H1 leads with their vision, not the contractor's name.

Social proof strip: Other homeowners in this city have been through this and are glad they called. Pull a review quote that uses the word "vision" or "transformation" if available.

Service split: Two buyer types, two CTAs. Maintenance buyers and design/install buyers must not share a single CTA. Never funnel both to the same page from the hero.

Project gallery: Specific named project types with cost ranges. The homeowner must be able to find a project that looks like what they want to do.

Deposit protection: Plain language only. "Your deposit is protected by a written contract" must appear in the H2 or directly below it. Do not bury this in body text.

Team: First names and real photos. "Real names and faces for everyone who will be on your property." This is the copy anchor for the section.

How it works: Three or four steps maximum. No step should be longer than two sentences. The homeowner must feel that the process is not mysterious.

FAQ: Write from the homeowner's 11pm Google search. Each question should be exactly what the homeowner types, not what the contractor wants to answer.

Final CTA: One low-friction ask. "Schedule a free yard assessment" or "See our before/after gallery." Never ask for more than a name, phone, and email on the first form.

---

## Anti-patterns (never do these for landscaping)

1. Stock photography. Real job photos only. If the client has no photos, halt at Stage 4 and request a photo shoot.
2. Generic About Us copy. Write specific: years in business, neighborhoods served, owner's name, what the owner cares about.
3. Buried phone number. Phone must be in the hero, in the navbar on mobile, and in the sticky mobile bar.
4. Skip deposit protection. It is non-negotiable. Every client gets this section regardless of whether they ask for it.
5. Single CTA for both buyer personas. Maintenance and design/install buyers need separate entry points.
