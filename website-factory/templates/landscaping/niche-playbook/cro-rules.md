# CRO Rules - Landscaping Niche

Conversion rate optimization patterns for residential landscaping websites. Based on niche research and analysis of sweeneyslandscaping.com (highest-scoring template site).

## Primary conversion path

Hero (dual CTAs) -> Gallery (social proof + desire) -> ServiceSplit (self-qualification) -> DepositProtection (risk removal) -> FinalCTA (form submit)

Every section should push the visitor toward the form at #lead-form or a phone call.

## Above-the-fold requirements

- Phone number must be visible without scrolling (in navbar on desktop, in hero subhead on all sizes)
- Primary CTA button must be visible without scrolling
- Trust signal (years in business, or review count) must be visible without scrolling

## Form rules

- 3 to 4 fields maximum on the primary lead form. Name, phone, email, project description (optional).
- Phone is required because landscaping is a phone-first conversion. Homeowners call, not email.
- Submit button copy uses the locked primary CTA text from copy-locks.json.
- Below the button: response time and privacy statement.
- After submit: show a thank-you message with the response time commitment.

## Mobile rules

- Sticky mobile bar (Call Now + Get a Quote) must persist on scroll on all pages.
- CTA buttons minimum 48px height.
- Phone number is always a tap-to-call link (href="tel:").
- Gallery is swipeable on mobile.

## Trust signal placement

- Google review aggregate above the fold or within first two sections.
- Deposit protection section between gallery and team (not buried in footer).
- License number and insurance in footer on every page.

## Gallery as a conversion driver

The gallery is the primary conversion driver for design/install buyers. Every gallery card must have:
- A project type label
- An investment range
- A neighborhood or area label

This specificity converts better than unattributed photos because it answers "can they do what I want, in my area, within my budget?" before the homeowner calls.

## Objection removal

The deposit protection section directly addresses the #1 objection in this niche: fear of losing a deposit. It must:
- Stand alone (not in footer, not in FAQ)
- Use direct language: "written contract", "scope", "timeline", "payment schedule"
- Be visible to every visitor who scrolls past the gallery
