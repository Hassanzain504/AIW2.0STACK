01

What is broken on the website

Every item here was found by crawling your live site. Nothing is estimated. Severity is ordered by what is costing you customers right now.

Critical
Your homepage is 7.6 MB. It should be under 1 MB.
Six images on the homepage are over 900 KB each, the largest is Commerical-Buildings.png at 1,297 KB. They are saved as PNG, which is a format meant for logos, not photographs. On a phone on mobile data this is the difference between your page appearing in about one second and about eight. Most people leave before it loads. Your hosting is not the problem, your server responds in 0.28 seconds. It is only the images.
Critical
CDC, EPA and OSHA logos are displayed on your homepage
All three are US federal agencies that restrict use of their seals. Showing them beside your certifications implies an endorsement that does not exist. Given you are pursuing federal contracting work, this is exactly the kind of thing that can disqualify a bidder. It takes ten minutes to remove. We would replace them with accurate wording, which is just as persuasive and is true: "Follows CDC and EPA disinfection guidance", "OSHA bloodborne pathogen compliant".
Critical
Google has no idea where your business is located
Your site carries no LocalBusiness structured data. There is no phone number, no address, no service area and no coordinates in a format Google can read. Your street address, 121 Talbott St, does not appear anywhere on the website at all, even though it is listed on your Chamber and HomeAdvisor profiles. For a business that lives or dies on local search, this is the cheapest fix with the largest effect.
Critical
You have four pages. You offer eleven services across three states.
Your entire site is the homepage, About, Contact and one shared Services page. That means nothing on your site can rank for "biohazard cleanup Montgomery County" or "medical facility cleaning Bethesda", because no page is about those things. Clean Method has 500 pages. Tri State Restorations has around 618.
High
Three of your four pages have no main heading
Services, About and Contact all have zero H1 tags. This is a common Elementor default that nobody changes. Your homepage does have one, but it reads "Precision Cleaning, Every Time", which is a tagline. It contains no service and no location, so it tells Google nothing.
High
Your homepage and services page are competing against each other
Homepage title: "Professional Cleaning Services in DMV | Black Ops Cleaning". Services page title: "Professional Cleaning Services DMV | Black Ops Cleaning Co." They are nearly identical, so Google has to guess which one to show and neither builds authority.
High
Your page descriptions sell residential cleaning. Your business does not.
The homepage description says "for homes and businesses" and the services page says "residential, commercial and specialized". But everything else on the site, your ISSA and GBAC certifications, your imagery, and your own positioning is commercial, medical and government. This pulls in homeowner enquiries you cannot serve profitably and confuses Google about what your company actually is. There is also a stray full stop mid-sentence: "Black Ops Cleaning. offers".
High
22 of your 32 homepage images have no description text
Empty alt attributes. On a page that is mostly images, that is a wasted signal to Google and a page that is unusable for anyone with a screen reader.
Medium
The two "map" links on your site point at the wrong place
The location link in your footer, on all three pages, resolves to google.com/maps/place/Rockville,+MD,+USA, which is the city of Rockville. A second one on the homepage resolves to the state of Virginia. Neither points at your business. Whoever built the site searched the city in Maps and pasted the share link.
Medium
Your contact form cannot qualify a commercial enquiry
It collects name, email and message, plus two fields that were never given labels. There is no phone field, no service type, no facility type and no square footage. For commercial cleaning that is the difference between a usable quote request and three emails of back and forth.
Medium
You advertise emergency response with 9 to 5 hours
Your site sells emergency cleaning and sewage decontamination. Your listed hours are Monday to Friday, 9:00 to 5:00. Sewage backups and biohazard incidents happen at night and at weekends. Every single biohazard competitor in this market advertises 24/7. Right now the highest margin calls in your entire service list are going to whoever picks up.
Medium
You cannot tell where a single lead came from
There is no call tracking on the site. One number appears everywhere, so there is no way to know whether a call came from Google, from Maps, from a truck or from a referral. Until that is fixed, no marketing spend can be judged.

Google Maps: the asset you do not have

This is the largest single gap in your marketing, and it is the one that can be closed fastest.

Our finding: you almost certainly have no Google Business Profile

We checked six different ways and found no business listing. The evidence:

  • The two map links on your website point at the city of Rockville and the state of Virginia, not at a business listing
  • Your site tells Google about your Facebook and Instagram, but lists no Google profile
  • There is no map embedded anywhere on the site, not even on the contact page
  • There is no "review us on Google" anywhere
  • You have zero reviews on Angi and zero on Facebook
  • Across six separate searches, no Google Maps listing for your company appeared

If that is right, then every search for a cleaning company near Rockville is currently showing a competitor's map listing and yours is not in the running. Not ranked low. Not present.

What that one job is worth

If it is a janitorial contract: mid-size commercial contracts in this market run roughly $1,000 to $1,500 a month, and they recur for as long as the contract holds. One contract signed in month three is still paying in month thirty.

If it is a specialty job: biohazard cleanup averages around $4,000 nationally, unattended death cleanup runs $2,000 to $7,000, and hoarding cleanup averages around $7,500. A single one of those covers this entire engagement outright.

These are published industry figures, not measurements from your business. We install call tracking in week one, so from month two this model is replaced by your real numbers.

What we would do with the profile

  • Create and verify it in week one. Verification can take weeks, so it starts immediately or it delays everything else.
  • Set the correct primary category. This is the strongest single field on a profile and we would match it against what the current top three in Rockville use.
  • Load all eleven services as separate items with proper descriptions, plus the Products section, which almost nobody in this market uses.
  • Twenty real photographs to start, then weekly uploads from your crew.
  • Seed and answer the questions buyers actually ask, before a competitor answers them for you.
  • Build the review routine and start with your existing clients.
  • Merge your duplicate Facebook page and fix your Chamber of Commerce listing, which currently returns an error page.
