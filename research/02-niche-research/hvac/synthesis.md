End customer in one sentence: A homeowner dealing with a broken system or aging unit who fears being upsold an unnecessary replacement, does not understand their options, and needs a contractor who will answer the phone today and tell them the truth.

The student's client in one sentence: A residential HVAC contractor running 2-5 technicians, doing $500K-$2M in annual revenue, buying shared leads from Angi or Thumbtack and losing margin to seasonality and low-ticket service calls.

Top 5 insights:

1. The $5,000 rule question dominates HVAC search. It appears in the People Also Ask box for every HVAC query analyzed. Homeowners are actively trying to make the repair-vs-replace decision without a contractor's input. The contractor whose website answers this honestly — with local cost data — gets the research visit before anyone else gets the phone call.

2. Individual technicians are the product, not the company. Reviews name technicians by first and last name. "Emmanuel Luna" appears in 5+ reviews. Homeowners trust the person on their property, not a logo. Technician profiles on the website convert the "who is coming to my home?" anxiety into a trust moment before the visit.

3. After-hours availability is a real differentiator. HVAC breakdowns do not respect business hours. Contractors who answer 24/7 and communicate that clearly on their site capture the emergency-intent homeowner before the competitor who has a voicemail. This is a low-cost operational choice with outsized conversion impact.

4. Maintenance plans create the most valuable customer in the niche. A maintenance agreement customer is worth $8,500-$10,000 over their lifetime (plan fees plus one replacement). The website must convert not just breakdown-intent visitors but maintenance-intent visitors, with a plan tier section and a specific CTA.

5. Seasonality is the contractor's biggest fear; the website can counteract it. Shoulder months (spring and fall) are when proactive homeowners schedule tune-ups and make replacement decisions before the emergency. A website that speaks directly to the shoulder-month homeowner ("Get your AC ready before the first hot week — here's what a tune-up includes") fills the schedule in slow periods.

Top 3 risks if we pick this niche:

- HVAC is dominated by large platforms like Scorpion with full-cycle technology stacks; competing on features is not viable for a student.
- The niche requires deep technical knowledge to write credible copy; shallow copy about "comfort" is immediately obvious to contractors and homeowners.
- Seasonality means HVAC contractors may pause or cancel website services in slow months unless they are on a maintenance retainer relationship.

Top 3 reasons to pick this niche:

- Replacement tickets of $7,000-$15,000 mean one extra closed job per month justifies the website investment immediately and completely.
- The $5,000 rule content gap is real and unoccupied by any contractor in the SERP; a student who produces this content for their clients gets quick organic traction.
- Maintenance plan upsell makes every client relationship a recurring revenue conversation; contractors who understand LTV are willing to invest in their website.

Money math headline: "An HVAC contractor who closes one extra system replacement per month from their own website recovers the entire site investment in the first job and builds a customer for 10 years of maintenance plans on top of that."
