### 3.1 Why this structure ranks

- One page per money keyword means each page can be laser-focused and fully optimized for
  a single term. This is how you beat a generalist competitor whose "web design" page tries
  to rank for everything at once.
- The blog feeds internal links into the money pages. Every blog post links up to the
  relevant service page with keyword-rich anchor text. That passes authority to the pages
  that make money.
- The portfolio and reviews pages carry your E-E-A-T (experience, expertise,
  authoritativeness, trust), which Google weights heavily for anyone selling a service.

### 3.2 On pricing

Contractor Gorilla hides pricing to force a sales call. You can win trust and long-tail
traffic by showing at least a starting price or a clear package structure, because
"how much does a window cleaning website cost" is a real search and a page that answers it
ranks and pre-sells. Recommended: a /pricing page with 2 to 3 named packages and a "starting
at" number, plus a note that final quotes are tailored. This is a judgment call tied to your
sales model, so decide deliberately. If you prefer to keep exact numbers off, still publish a
page that explains what drives cost and gives a range, so you capture the search.

### 3.3 The lead magnet

/free-website-audit is your highest-value page. It offers a free audit of a window cleaning
company's current site (which you already produce with your outreach tool's checklist). It

## 4. On-page SEO blueprint (per page type)

For every page, fill in these elements. This is the on-page checklist that makes a page
eligible to rank. Do all of them on every page. No exceptions.

### 4.1 Universal on-page checklist (every page)

- [ ] Title tag: 50 to 60 characters, primary keyword first, brand at the end.
      Format: "Primary Keyword | Wolf Contractor"
- [ ] Meta description: 140 to 155 characters, includes the keyword and a reason to click
      (a benefit + a soft CTA). Google may rewrite it, write it anyway.
- [ ] One H1 only, containing the primary keyword, phrased for humans.
- [ ] H2 and H3 subheads that use secondary and question keywords naturally.
- [ ] URL slug is short, lowercase, hyphenated, keyword-first, no stop words.
- [ ] First 100 words contain the primary keyword and state clearly what the page offers.
- [ ] At least 3 internal links out to related pages with descriptive anchor text.
- [ ] At least one internal link IN from another page (nothing is an orphan).
- [ ] Primary image has a descriptive filename and alt text with the keyword where honest.
- [ ] One clear primary CTA above the fold and repeated at the bottom (book a call / get audit).
- [ ] Schema markup present (Section 4.4).
- [ ] Word count deep enough to fully answer intent (money pages 900 to 1,500 words, blog
      posts 1,200 to 2,000, comparison pages 1,200+). Never pad; depth beats length.
- [ ] Mobile layout checked; tap targets and phone number are tappable.

### 4.2 Money page template (e.g. /window-cleaning-website-design)

Section order that ranks and converts:

1. H1 with the keyword, plus a one-line value proposition.
2. Primary CTA (book a call) and a trust strip (reviews count, projects, guarantee).
3. The problem, stated in the owner's language (a slow, generic, no-booking website is
   losing them jobs). This uses the exact pains from your audit checklist: no hard-water
   page, no before/after photos, no reviews shown, no instant quote.
4. What you deliver, as scannable benefits tied to bookings, not features.
5. Proof: 2 to 3 mini case studies or before/after screenshots with a real metric.
6. What is included / packages (link to /pricing).
7. Process in 3 to 4 steps (link to /process).
8. FAQ block, 5 to 8 questions, each an H3 that matches a real search question. This block
   is what wins AI answers and the "People also ask" box.
9. Final CTA.

### 4.3 The FAQ block (do this on every money page and the pricing page)

FAQs are the single highest-leverage on-page element for both Google's "People also ask"
and AI answer engines, because they are question-and-answer shaped, which is exactly what
those systems extract. For each money page, write 5 to 8 real questions a window cleaning
owner asks, answered in 40 to 60 words each, in plain language. Examples for the website
design page:

- How much does a window cleaning website cost?
- How long does it take to build a window cleaning website?
- Will my new website help me get more bookings?
- Do you write the content or do I?
- Can you help me rank on Google too?
- What makes a window cleaning website convert visitors into jobs?

Mark them up with FAQPage schema (Section 4.4).

### 4.4 Schema markup (structured data)

Add JSON-LD to the site. This is what earns rich results and helps AI engines parse you.

- Organization + LocalBusiness on the homepage (name Wolf Contractor, logo, URL, sameAs
  links to your social profiles, contact point).
- Service schema on each money page (serviceType = the keyword, provider = Wolf Contractor,
  areaServed = United States).
- FAQPage schema wrapping each FAQ block.
- Review / AggregateRating schema on /reviews and in testimonial blocks (only with real
  reviews; never fake this, it violates Google policy and can get you penalized).
- BreadcrumbList schema on deep pages.
- Article schema on every blog post (headline, author, datePublished, dateModified).

Validate every page in Google's Rich Results Test before you consider it done.

- [ ] Write the FAQ block (5 to 8 Q&A) for every money page and the pricing page.

## 5. Technical SEO

If the site is slow, unindexable, or messy, none of the content work matters. Get these
right once, then monitor.

### 5.1 Crawl and index

- [ ] Register the site in Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools.
- [ ] Generate and submit an XML sitemap (sitemap.xml). Most site builders and frameworks
      do this automatically; confirm it exists and is submitted in Search Console.
- [ ] Add a robots.txt that allows crawling and points to the sitemap.
- [ ] Ensure every important page returns HTTP 200 and is not accidentally set to noindex.
- [ ] Set one canonical version of the domain (pick www or non-www, https only) and 301
      redirect the others. Never let both resolve.
- [ ] Fix any broken internal links and 404s. Redirect old URLs to live ones with 301.

### 5.2 Speed and Core Web Vitals

Google ranks fast pages higher and users bounce off slow ones. Targets:

- Largest Contentful Paint under 2.5 seconds.
- Interaction to Next Paint under 200 ms.
- Cumulative Layout Shift under 0.1.

How to hit them:

- [ ] Compress and serve images in modern formats (WebP or AVIF). Portfolio screenshots are
      your heaviest assets; size them properly.
- [ ] Lazy-load below-the-fold images.
- [ ] Minimize third-party scripts. Every chat widget and tracker costs speed.
- [ ] Use a CDN and good hosting. If the site is on Vercel or similar, this is mostly handled.
- [ ] Test each key page in PageSpeed Insights and fix what it flags.

### 5.3 Mobile and crawlable content

- [ ] Fully responsive; Google indexes the mobile version first.
- [ ] Text is real HTML text, not baked into images, so it can be read and quoted.
- [ ] If the site is a JavaScript single-page app, confirm Google can render the content
      (use the URL Inspection tool's rendered view). Server-side render or pre-render the
      money pages if there is any doubt. This matters a lot for AI crawlers, which are worse
      at JavaScript than Google.

### 5.4 Security and housekeeping

- [ ] HTTPS everywhere with a valid certificate.
- [ ] Clean, readable URLs (no query-string junk for main pages).
- [ ] A custom 404 page that links back to key pages.

### ACTIONS 5

- [ ] Complete the crawl-and-index checklist in week 1; nothing else works until the site
      is indexable.
- [ ] Run every money page through PageSpeed Insights and fix until Core Web Vitals pass.
- [ ] Confirm content is rendered as real text and reachable without JavaScript execution.

## 7. Backlink strategy (off-page authority)

Backlinks are still one of the strongest ranking factors. You want links that are relevant
(home-services, small-business, marketing, or window-cleaning context) and real. A handful
of relevant links beats a hundred spammy ones. Never buy link packages from marketplaces;
they are the fastest way to a penalty.

### 7.1 Foundation links (do first, easy, safe)

- [ ] Your own profiles: LinkedIn company page, Facebook business page, Instagram, X,
      YouTube, plus the About/founder profiles. Link each back to the site. These are the
      "sameAs" entries in your schema too.
- [ ] Business directories in 7.3. Consistent NAP.
- [ ] Google Business Profile (from Section 6) links to the site.

### 7.2 Authority and relevance links (the ones that move rankings)

- [ ] Guest posts on window-cleaning and home-service industry blogs and communities. You
      already know this industry cold from your audits; pitch "how window cleaners can get
      more bookings from their website." Link back to a money page.
- [ ] Podcasts and YouTube channels aimed at window cleaning and pressure washing business
      owners (there is an active community: WCRA, window cleaning business coaches, pressure
      washing YouTubers). Get interviewed. Each show notes page is a relevant link plus
      buyer eyeballs.
- [ ] Industry associations: International Window Cleaning Association (IWCA) and regional
      groups. A member or partner listing is a strong, relevant link.
- [ ] HARO-style and journalist request services (Help a B2B Writer, Featured, Qwoted).
      Answer questions about small-business websites and marketing; earn links from real
      publications.
- [ ] Free-tool / resource links: your /free-website-audit and any calculator (e.g. a
      "window cleaning website cost calculator") are linkable assets. Pitch them to
      industry newsletters and directories of small-business tools.
- [ ] The comparison and "alternative" pages naturally attract links from people discussing
      vendor choices in forums and communities.

### 7.3 Directory and citation targets (foundation)

Google Business Profile, Bing Places, Apple Business Connect, Yelp, Clutch, DesignRush,
The Manifest, GoodFirms, UpCity, Trustpilot, Better Business Bureau, LinkedIn, Facebook,
Crunchbase, and any local chamber of commerce. Clutch, DesignRush, and UpCity matter

### 7.4 Competitor backlink mining

### 7.5 Anchor text discipline

Keep anchor text natural and varied. Mix branded ("Wolf Contractor"), naked URL, and only

## 10. AI ranking (AEO / GEO): getting cited by ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews

This is the "AI ranking" you asked for. When an owner asks ChatGPT or Perplexity "who
builds websites for window cleaning companies" or "best window cleaning marketing agency,"
you want Wolf Contractor named. This is a real, winnable channel and most competitors are
not optimizing for it yet, so being early is an edge.

### 10.1 How AI answers pick who to mention

AI answer engines pull from the live web (and their training data). They favor content that:

- Directly answers the question in clear, quotable sentences near the top of the page.
- Is structured as questions and answers, lists, and tables (easy to extract).
- Comes from a site that appears authoritative and is mentioned consistently across the web
  (reviews, directories, listicles, forum mentions). AI engines lean heavily on third-party
  "best of" lists and reputable directories.
- Is technically crawlable as plain text (see Section 5.3; AI crawlers are weak at
  JavaScript).

### 10.2 What to do

- [ ] Answer-first writing: every money page and blog post states the direct answer in the
      first sentence or two, then elaborates. "A window cleaning website typically costs X
      to Y and should include Z" beats a slow intro.
- [ ] FAQ and Q&A blocks everywhere (already in Section 4.3). This is the most extractable
      format for AI.
- [ ] Get listed on the third-party "best window cleaning marketing agency" and "best
      contractor web design" listicles and directories (Clutch, DesignRush, UpCity, plus
      niche blog roundups). AI engines quote these lists when asked "who is best." Reach out
      to the authors of existing roundups and ask to be included with a real case study.
- [ ] Publish your own honest roundup ("Best marketing agencies for window cleaners in
      2026") that includes competitors and yourself with clear selection criteria. These get
      cited by AI and rank well, and being the source of the list is powerful.
- [ ] Keep your name, positioning, and facts (projects, years, niche) consistent across the
      whole web so the AI's picture of you is coherent.
- [ ] Do not block AI crawlers in robots.txt if you want to be cited. Decide deliberately:
      to be surfaced in AI answers, allow GPTBot, PerplexityBot, Google-Extended, and
      ClaudeBot. (Blocking them protects content from training but removes you from those
      answers.)
- [ ] Add clear, factual "about" data: a strong /about page, Organization schema, and
      consistent social profiles give AI a reliable entity to attach facts to.
- [ ] Test monthly: literally ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and Google's AI Overview the
      target questions ("best window cleaning website design company," "Contractor Gorilla
      alternative," "who builds websites for window cleaners"). Note whether you appear and
      what sources it cites. Then go earn a presence on those exact sources.

### ACTIONS 10

- [ ] Rewrite the top of every money page to answer-first.

## 11. Measurement and tools

You cannot improve what you do not track. Set these up before you start so you have a
baseline.

Free and essential:

- Google Search Console: impressions, clicks, positions, indexing, and the exact queries
  you rank for. This is the single most important tool. Check weekly.
- Google Analytics 4: traffic and conversions (calls booked, audit requests).
- Bing Webmaster Tools: index coverage and Bing/Copilot visibility.
- Google Business Profile insights: local views and actions.
- PageSpeed Insights and Rich Results Test: technical validation.

Paid or freemium (pick one keyword/backlink tool):

- Ahrefs or Semrush (best, paid) for keyword volume, rank tracking, and competitor backlinks.
- Ubersuggest or the free tiers if budget is tight to start.

What to track and the honest timeline:

- Week 0: record baseline (rankings, traffic, indexed pages, backlinks).
- Month 1: pages indexed, technical health green, first content live.
- Month 2 to 3: long-tail and low-competition keywords start ranking; first leads from search.
- Month 3 to 6: money pages climbing; steady lead flow; AI mentions begin appearing.
- Month 6 to 12: top-3 positions on the core window-cleaning cluster; compounding traffic.

SEO is slow and compounding. Anyone promising page one in two weeks for competitive terms
is lying. The plan above is built to be durable, not to spike and collapse.
