## Decision Moments (when the homeowner is making a choice)

1. **The inciting event.** A leak appears. A storm hits. A home inspection flags the roof. The homeowner goes from passive to actively searching.

2. **The search.** "Roofing contractor near me" or "emergency roof repair." BBB, Angi, and Thumbtack appear before individual contractor sites. The contractor website is compared against aggregators.

3. **The quote visit.** A rep arrives. How they dress, how they explain the issue, whether they go on the roof or just glance from the driveway: all are evaluated. Honesty in this moment is the top trust driver based on review language.

4. **The insurance conversation.** For storm or hail damage, the contractor's ability to navigate the claim process is itself a selling point. Homeowners feel out of their depth with insurance adjusters; contractors who help feel like allies.

5. **The job.** Crew behavior on the property, whether debris is cleaned up, whether the project manager communicates delays. These drive the review outcome.

6. **Post-job.** Whether the fix holds. Whether they answer when called back. Warranty communication and follow-through drive referrals.

---

## Top 5 Fears (by frequency, intensity, and addressability)

1. **Being overcharged or scammed.** The homeowner has no way to independently assess roof condition. They depend on the contractor's honesty. The fear of being told "you need a full replacement" when a repair would do is acute. Addressable via transparent pricing, itemized quotes, and review volume.

2. **Poor workmanship that fails later.** Specific fear: coating that cracks, a leak returning, damage caused by the crew. The "3-year-old crumbling recoat" review is the nightmare scenario. Addressable via warranties, before/after photos, and material specifics.

3. **Not being kept informed.** Homeowners who have had bad experiences describe being ghosted after deposit or not being told of delays. Addressable via communication SLAs shown on the website ("We call you the day before, the morning of, and after every job").

4. **Choosing the wrong contractor from many options.** The homeowner is comparing three or more quotes. Without expertise, they default to reviews and brand signals. Addressable via third-party review count, photo galleries, and specific certifications.

5. **Insurance claim complexity.** Post-storm homeowners face adjusters, timelines, and paperwork. Contractors who visibly offer insurance claim assistance reduce a specific, high-intensity fear. Addressable via a dedicated insurance claim section on the website.
