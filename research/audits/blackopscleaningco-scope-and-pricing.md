# Scope of Work and Pricing: Black Ops Cleaning

Internal working doc. Everything that needs doing, how hard each item is, how long it takes,
what to charge, and how to grow the account past the first engagement.

Currency: USD, because the client is US based.

Effort ratings: **Easy** = mechanical, you can do it fast. **Medium** = needs thought or
writing. **Hard** = depends on the client, or on Google, or takes real skill.

## Part 3. How to grow this account

The first engagement is the door. This client has an unusual number of genuine upsells because
of what they actually do.

### Tier 1. Sell in months 1 to 3

| Service | Price | Why it lands |
|---|---|---|
| Additional service pages | $225 to $350 each | Thirteen exist on the list, sprint only covers 10 |
| Additional town pages | $165 to $250 each | Twelve towns mapped, sprint only covers 4 |
| Photography day | $400 to $700 | They have zero real photos and desperately need them |

### Tier 2. Sell in months 4 to 6

| Service | Price | Why it lands |
|---|---|---|
| **Government contracting page + capability statement** | **$700 to $1,200** | See below. This is the biggest one. |
| Review management as a managed add-on | $250 to $400/mo | If the founder will not make the calls, sell doing it for them |
| Blog content, 2 posts a month | $300 to $500/mo | Cost content ranks and feeds AI answers |
| Local Falcon geogrid reporting tier | $100 to $200/mo | Upgrade the reporting, upgrade the fee |

**On the government angle, which is the strongest upsell here.** They are already registered in
SAM.gov (UEI PMPPFTB7CCX4) and they appear in GovTribe, G2Xchange and Pegbo. They are further
along in federal contracting than their website suggests. What they are missing is the
front end: a page that targets government facility contracting, a proper capability statement
PDF, NAICS alignment, and the SDVOSB or VOSB certification finished. In the DMV that is not
marketing, it is a procurement channel, and set-aside contracts are legally restricted to
certified firms. Sell this as a distinct project, not as part of SEO.

### Tier 3. Sell in months 6 to 12

| Service | Price | Why it lands |
|---|---|---|
| Google Ads or Local Services Ads management | $500 to $900/mo + spend | Once organic proves the lead value |
| Email and quote follow-up automation | $600 to $1,200 setup | Commercial cleaning has a long sales cycle, most quotes die from no follow-up |
| Second location GBP (if they open one) | $600 to $900 | Doubles their map pack coverage |
| Video: job walkthroughs for YouTube and service pages | $500 to $1,000 | Ranks, embeds, and nobody in this trade does it |
| Quarterly strategy retainer upgrade | +$300 to $600/mo | Once they trust you with more than SEO |

### The retention mechanic

Two things keep this account for years:

1. **Two visible pages every month.** The client sees a deliverable land whether or not
   rankings moved that month. This is why town pages are the perfect retainer filler: there are
   twelve of them, they are repeatable, and each one is obviously new work.
2. **A monthly report that ends on revenue, not rankings.** Geogrid before and after, GBP calls,
   leads by source, and then quotes and jobs won. Rankings are the method. Jobs are the point.

## Part 4. What is easy versus what is hard

Sort your own time by this. Batch the easy work, charge properly for the hard work.

**Easy, batch it, high margin:** image compression, alt text, H1 fixes, citations, directory
claims, GBP photo uploads, GBP posts, logo removal, adding the address.

**Medium, where the craft is:** page writing, service item descriptions, schema, title and meta
rewrites, form design, review templates.

**Hard, price it accordingly, and do not promise timelines you do not control:** GBP creation
and verification, review generation (depends entirely on the founder), anything that depends on
Google's timing, and the positioning decision (depends on the client deciding).

**Not your job, and say so out loud:** getting reviews, taking job photos, answering the phone
24/7, and deciding what business they are. Put these in the contract as client obligations. If
they are not delivered, the results do not arrive, and you need that in writing before month
three, not during it.

## Part 5. Two things to confirm before you quote

1. **Does a Google Business Profile exist?** Search the exact business name in Google Maps.
   Every signal says it does not: the site's two Maps links point at the city of Rockville and
   the state of Virginia rather than a business, the schema lists only Facebook and Instagram,
   there is no map embed, and there are zero reviews on every platform checked. If it does not
   exist, add the $300 and start verification in week 1, because it gates everything.

2. **Will they answer the phone 24/7?** Every biohazard competitor in the market advertises
   24/7. If the answer is no, the emergency and specialty pages will rank and not convert, and
   the scope should shrink accordingly. Ask in week 1.
